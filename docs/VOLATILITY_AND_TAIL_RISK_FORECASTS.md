# Volatility and liquidation-tail forecasts: risk skill is not direction skill

Reviewed 2026-10-19. This note proposes a separate risk-forecasting lane and reviews evidence relevant to it. It adds no result to the project's return forecasts and does not change a frozen experiment.

## Keep the target separate from R209

R209 tests whether the difference between Deribit implied volatility and trailing realized volatility times BTC/ETH perpetual exposure. Its signal is a volatility-risk-premium timing rule. A different question is whether information at time `t` forecasts the next interval's realized variance, or whether a forecast lets a fixed book keep risk closer to a declared budget. That second result would help estimate exposure risk; it would not predict a coin's return sign or establish positive expected PnL.

Also separate three outcomes: (1) future realized variance or a tail-price move, (2) a hypothetical position crossing a specified margin boundary, and (3) an actual account liquidation. The latter requires the account's collateral, margin mode, tier, funding, order and forced-close records. Public price bars can support the first, and venue rules plus disclosed hypothetical positions can support the second; neither identifies the third.

## What the evidence supports

**Simple realized-volatility forecasts are serious baselines.** Bergsli et al. compare realized-volatility HAR models with GARCH-family forecasts on BTC/USD Bitstamp data from 2014–2018. HAR models using intraday realized variance outperform GARCH models based on daily returns in their comparison, especially at short horizons, but the ranking varies with horizon and loss. Brauneis and Sahiner's eight-coin Coinbase study likewise finds model rankings vary by coin and loss; Bitcoin is an exception to their broad machine-learning-over-HAR result. These studies motivate a small horizon-matched horse race, not a universal winning model or a MEXC perpetual conclusion ([Bergsli et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ribaf.2021.101540); [Brauneis & Sahiner 2024](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10690-024-09510-6)).

Corsi's parsimonious HAR-RV benchmark separates daily, weekly and monthly realized volatility. For a 24/7 asset, define those as trailing 1, 7 and 30 calendar days rather than importing equity trading-day windows. Gkillas, Gupta and Pierdzioch report that adding tail-shape measures can improve forecasts when underprediction is penalized more heavily, particularly at shorter horizons; this supports reporting downside misses separately ([Corsi 2009](https://doi.org/10.1093/jjfinec/nbp001); [Gkillas et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1080/1351847X.2021.1906728)).

**Options are horizon-dependent volatility comparators.** Hoang and Baur find Deribit BTC implied volatility less accurate than ARMA/HAR at one day but more accurate at 7, 10 and 15 days; combining implied volatility with model forecasts performs best. Chi and Hao reach a different result for BTC and ETH in 2017–2020, where GARCH beats option IV. The disagreement is a reason to compare IV with simple forecasts at the same horizon and venue clock, not to assume the option market is always superior ([Hoang & Baur 2020](https://doi.org/10.1002/fut.22144); [Chi & Hao 2021](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.intfin.2021.101421)). BTC option-surface slopes have been found to forecast weekly realized volatility without forecasting ordinary returns. The same paper studies IV-slope responses to negative and positive jumps; that event result is not a directional forecast ([Chen, Deng & Nie 2024](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.orl.2024.107135)). Deribit's DVOL is a 30-day implied-volatility index built from option order books; timestamped option IV is available from its API. Historical index candles are not the same thing as complete historical option-chain surfaces, so do not infer that old skew or term-structure features can be reconstructed from DVOL alone ([DVOL methodology](https://insights.deribit.com/exchange-updates/dvol-deribit-implied-volatility-index/); [Deribit volatility-index API](https://docs.deribit.com/api-reference/market-data/public-get_volatility_index_data)).

**Volatility forecast ranking depends on measurement and loss.** Realized variance is a noisy proxy for latent variance; Patton shows that some forecast-comparison losses can rank models differently when the target is measured imperfectly. Use a declared robust loss such as QLIKE as the primary comparison, retain a second loss and calibration summaries, and report the realized-variance construction and sampling frequency ([Patton 2011](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jeconom.2010.03.034)).

**Margin-tail forecasts are path forecasts, not return alpha.** Pindza and Mashele compare a structural first-passage model with trailing historical simulation for hypothetical isolated-margin breaches on Binance BTC, ETH and SOL perpetuals. In their short sample, the structural model has lower 24-, 72- and 168-hour Brier scores at 10x; historical simulation does better at one week at 20x and 50x, where 51/63 and 60/63 overlapping forecast origins breach. The paper's 63–81 daily-spaced origins, Gaussian innovations, fixed maintenance assumptions and Binance-only setting make these descriptive evidence, not a validated MEXC liquidation model. A separate ten-asset Binance study reports open-interest change as its strongest crash predictor but an out-of-sample AUC of 0.677; treat OI as a candidate risk-state variable with limited discrimination, not as a stand-alone alarm ([Pindza & Mashele 2026](https://doi.org/10.3390/forecast8050076); [Tran et al. 2026](https://doi.org/10.1108/SEF-04-2026-0367)).

## Proposed new lane, after the current frozen work

Before computing a new score, register a fresh lane and leave R209's inputs and holdout untouched. Freeze a base portfolio, point-in-time eligible contracts, venue price family, decision/receipt clock, horizon, return construction, missing-data handling, and the exact forecast output. For a first, tractable question, forecast the next 24-hour realized variance of a fixed, unscaled portfolio using MEXC fair-price returns; keep actual PnL, funding and fees in a separate account-outcome ledger.

Compare persistence from the last completed day, trailing 7-/30-day realized variance, HAR-RV and a daily-return GARCH baseline. Add a volatility index or fixed-tenor option IV only if its data timestamp and history are available at each decision time. Estimate every model recursively or with past-only rolling fits. Use QLIKE versus a named baseline as the primary forecast score, plus calibration, underforecast frequency/severity and coverage. Treat all coins on one date as a shared market observation: use date-block uncertainty for a portfolio score, not independent coin-row standard errors.

Only after forecast scoring is frozen should a second utility test apply a common capped, no-leverage volatility scaler to the same base book. Compare it with constant sizing and a trailing-volatility scaler after fees and funding. Forecast portfolio covariance or book-level variance; separate coin-by-coin volatility does not account for common shocks. Do not call a variance forecast VaR or expected shortfall without separately validating distribution shape and tail coverage. A better risk forecast can support safer sizing even if it has no return-prediction skill.

For liquidation risk, create another outcome family: price adverse excursion, hypothetical venue-rule margin breach for fixed disclosed positions, and actual account liquidation only when account events are observed. Capture fair/mark, index and last prices, funding, versioned contract/risk tiers, and first local receipt. Compare historical simulation with prespecified volatility, jump or extreme-value alternatives using Brier/log scores for breach probabilities, quantile loss and exceedance checks for tails. Account for overlapping horizons and clustered events. MEXC's [margin guide](https://www.mexc.com/support/article/mexc-margin-calculation-guide-312054740132052992), [risk-limit endpoint](https://www.mexc.co/api-docs/futures/account-and-trading-endpoints/get-risk-limits) and [price definitions](https://www.mexc.com/support/article/faq-on-index-price-fair-price-and-last-price-7950960183961) describe venue mechanics but do not supply the owner's realized account path.

## Synthetic sizing check only

For a 1% daily risk target and a 2% forecast volatility, inverse-volatility scaling gives 0.5x exposure. If actual volatility is 4%, the scaled book realizes about 2% risk, twice the target. The arithmetic illustrates forecast error transmission; it is not a market estimate or a strategy result.

No new market data, forecast score, backtest or margin-breach statistic was computed for this note. R209 and all frozen lanes remain unchanged.
