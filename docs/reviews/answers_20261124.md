## 1. What maintenance margin does Kraken apply to an EU client on our coins?
- **Source:** Kraken support, "Derivatives margin schedule & maximum leverage for EEA clients" (page read 2026-11-24).
  - Class E, Level I (positions of $0-250,000): 10x, initial margin 10%, **maintenance margin 5%**.
  - Level II: 5x (20% / 10%); Level III: 3.33x (30% / 15%); Level IV: 2x (50% / 25%).
  - Every coin R509 held is Class E on Kraken's EEA contract list (updated 2026-06-29).
  - The page sets no separate retail schedule. Its "retail client" sentence is the Australian disclaimer.
- **What it settles.** The API's 50%/25% retail schedule (`dlt`) is another entity's. For our positions, far under
  $250,000 a contract, Kraken EU's maintenance is 5%.
- **Decision.** The Kraken lane may run at **1.5x**.
  - At 1.5x and 5%, the all-legs-against distance is 59%. On 2025-10-10, Kraken's own mark fell −43.8% (mean of 126
    coins) and −62.8% at worst.
  - I keep alerts and a daily rebalance. 2x (43%) is not used.

## 2. How are the gains taxed for a French resident?
- **Source:** BOFiP BOI-RPPM-PVBMI-70-10 and -70-20, in force since 2019-12-20 (read 2026-11-24).
  - **Art. 150 ter CGI** covers occasional profits on forward financial instruments: "contrats financiers avec
    paiement d'un différentiel", and "tout autre contrat à terme… qui présente les caractéristiques d'autres
    instruments financiers à terme".
  - **Profit per contract** = sums received − sums paid, so funding is part of it. The tax falls on the year's net
    across contracts.
  - **Rate:** the flat rate of art. 200 A (12.8% income tax) by default, or the progressive scale on option, plus
    social contributions (CSS art. L.136-6). Secondary sources give a 2026 total of 31.4%; I use 31.4%.
  - **Losses** offset profits of the same kind in the same year or the next ten years, never global income.
  - **Exception:** if the account keeper is in a non-cooperative state (art. 238-0 A), other than those of 2° of
    2 bis, the profit is taxed at a punitive rate.
- **The non-cooperative list** (order of 2026-04-15, as summarised by Deloitte and Mayer Brown; not read at the
  Journal officiel):
  - full measures: Antigua and Barbuda, Vanuatu, Anguilla, Turks and Caicos;
  - partial measures only: Guam, US Virgin Islands, Palau, Panama, Russia, American Samoa, Vietnam.
  - The British Virgin Islands (Bitfinex's iFinex) and the UAE (Deribit, owned by Coinbase) are not on it.
  - Hyperliquid has no account keeper.
- **Decision.**
  - Every return figure for the owner is shown after 31.4% tax on the year's net.
  - The two legs of a hedge are netted, because they are the same kind of profit.
  - Each foreign exchange account must be declared on form 3916-bis (an owner act, listed for them). A self-custody
    wallet (Hyperliquid) is not an account at an institution.
- **Assumed:** that perps on unregulated venues also fall under art. 150 ter rather than under BNC for a habitual
  activity. A daily-rebalanced book run by hand is still "occasional" for an individual without professional means;
  that is my reading, not a ruling.

## 3. What is the owner's cash hurdle?
- **The lab used the US 13-week bill (4.13%).** The owner is a French resident with euros.
- **Euro cash:**
  - Livret A is tax-free but capped;
  - a euro money-market rate of about 2% (ECB deposit rate) gives about 1.4% after 31.4% tax.
- **Decision.** The after-tax hurdle is **about 1.5%/yr in euros.** The strategy is compared after the same tax.
  - On the three forward pairs at 1x in sample: +6.7% × (1 − 0.314) ≈ +4.6% after tax.
  - Less the venue risk of about 2%: ≈ +2.6%, still above 1.5%.
- **A risk I had not seen: currency.** Collateral in USDC is a dollar position. For a euro investor its EUR/USD swing
  (7-10%/yr volatility) is larger than the book's own volatility (about 2%).
- **Decision.**
  - On Kraken, use euro collateral where its multi-collateral accepts it.
  - Elsewhere, keep only the USDC the margin needs.
  - Report results in euros.
  - Hedging the dollar leg (a short EUR/USD perp or future) is a later refinement.

## 4. What does "breakthrough" mean here, in money and time?
- **Decision.** A breakthrough is a book, proven on forward paper and then on a small real stake, whose after-tax
  excess over euro cash, times the capital it can absorb, reaches **€10,000 a year**, with a worst month above −5%.
- **Where we stand against it:**
  - Kraken's thin coins absorb about $40-100k. At a halved edge (+11-15%/yr), after tax and cash, that is roughly
    €3-8k a year: a sleeve, not yet the breakthrough.
  - The liquid pairs absorb millions, but earn little over cash after tax and venue risk.
- **Revision 2026-11-25** ([decay](reflection_20261125_decay.md)):
  - R508's return follows realized cross-venue dispersion, which fell by about a factor of 3 through 2026.
  - At the autumn level, the halved forward edge is about 6%/yr at 1x, about +2.5%/yr over euro cash after tax.
  - The €3-8k above was built on the whole year and overstates the current level by two to three times.
  - The sleeve pays most in sell-offs.
- **So the route** is several capacity rents of that kind, uncorrelated, each a few thousand euros a year. That is
  the mechanism map's job (`docs/reviews/mechanism_map.md`). It is a better use of time than one bigger idea.
