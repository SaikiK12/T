# How I approach a subject: a global reflection, 2026-11-24

- **The owner, 2026-11-24:** "Tu travailles trop et tu réfléchis peu sur toi-même. Tu es obligé de t'aider toi-même à
  avoir une meilleure réflexion globale sur la façon d'aborder tes sujets. C'est ça qui nous sépare terriblement des
  breakthroughs, et du coup tu dois passer le plus clair de ton temps à faire ça."
- **What this page is.** It is not a result. It is about how I think, and it is revised whenever the thinking
  changes.

## 1. What the record says about where value came from
- **The lab ran about 9.8 million trials in about 280 rounds.** One family replicates and is heading to money: the
  funding-design gap.
- **It did not come from testing.** It came from reading how venues compute funding (premium plus a clamped interest
  term) and noticing that some venues leave the interest term out. Then: who pays because of that difference, and why
  can nobody take it all? The test only confirmed what the reading had found.
- **Everything else fails or adds no edge:**
  - pattern mining (charts, sessions, flows, ML): 0-23% of decided tests pass;
  - published anomalies (R528 closing momentum, R529 index roll): null.
  - The risk and sizing work of the last week was necessary, but it is defence. It protects an edge and finds none.
- **The breakthrough was understanding a rule that others had not compared across venues.** Testing was the cheap
  last step.

## 2. What separates us from the next breakthrough (honest)
1. **I am a production line.** The two-hour loop asks for a "finished increment", so I pick tasks that finish in two
   hours: a tool, a note, a cheap test. Deep understanding does not finish in two hours, so it never gets picked.
2. **I search locally.** Each choice follows the last result (risk after the edge, sizing after risk). Nobody does the
   global search for the next mechanism.
3. **I test before I understand.** In the wide cycles I took other people's published ideas: public, crowded, already
   paid to someone faster. I should have looked for mechanisms that we see and others do not.
4. **I have no model of the competition.** Why is the design gap not arbitraged away? I believe the answers are split
   margin across venues, venue risk, KYC geography and thin coins, but I never wrote them down. Those same barriers
   are where the next edges hide.
5. **My self-improvement has been procedural.** After each mistake I added a rule. `CLAUDE.md` grew; my thinking did
   not deepen. Rules prevent repeated errors. They do not produce insight.
6. **I never re-asked what "breakthrough" means for this owner.**
   - A $2,000 stake at +20%/yr is $400.
   - The owner's real advantages are patience, small size, retail-only venues, and reading the rules of venues that
     funds ignore.
   - What turns that into a result that matters (capacity, compounding, several uncorrelated payers) is a thinking
     question I have not worked on.

## 3. The approach I will use on every subject (the method, not a checklist)
Before any test, I write one page that answers these in order:
1. **The economic question, in one sentence.** Not "does pattern X work".
2. **The mechanism.**
   - Who trades here, under which constraint (mandate, margin, regulation, deadline, attention)?
   - Which price is set by a formula or a rule rather than by trading?
   - Who must trade at a bad price, and why?
3. **The barrier.** Who could arbitrage it, and what stops them (capital split across venues, venue risk, access,
   size, attention)? No barrier means someone faster is paid first (L032).
4. **Size by Fermi estimate.** The forced flow × the price distortion = money a year. What is the capacity, and how
   much of it can we reach?
5. **Our edge in taking it.** What do we see or reach that others do not? If nothing, stop.
6. **The cheapest decisive observation:** a venue's API field, a doc, an hour of data, before any registered test.
7. **Only then a test,** with the forecast written first.

## 4b. The barrier, made precise (He, Manela, Ross & von Wachter, read 2026-11-24)
- **Funding above cash is the price end users pay for leverage.** It persists only where arbitrage capacity W/m (capital
  over margin per dollar intermediated) is small next to demand.
- **So a capacity rent needs all three:**
  1. high leverage demand: extrapolative retail on thin coins;
  2. small W/m: no netting across venues, thin books, venues funds avoid;
  3. our access.
- **Decay is the default.** As capital grows, the rent shrinks (−22 points a year in the paper). Every rent gets a
  quarterly reassessment, not a permanent allocation.

## 4c. Each row starts from the lab's own record (cycle 4)
- **The lab's 280 rounds are the first primary source.** Before reading a venue's rules for a map row, list the
  rounds that touched the mechanism and what they found. Read them as evidence about the mechanism, not as verdicts.
- **Why this matters.** After three cycles the map still had no row for the closed-market oracle. Meanwhile the lab
  held eleven rounds on it, two forward lanes, and its second-best payer family.
- **A failed replication can be the mechanism's control.**
  - R464 (index perps) failed where the mechanism says it should: index markets are hedgeable and quoted on many
    venues.
  - Classify a family by what forces the transfer, not by the name of the test. Then write the refined mechanism's
    new prediction, and test it on fresh data only.

## 4d. Rents decay, so the product is the pipeline (cycle 8)
- **Measured on the lab's longest panel** (R525, 12 pairs, 2021-2026): regime-adjusted, the family loses about 30%
  of its mean level a year. The old liquid pairs have reached zero. Only the newest pairs (Kraken's thin coins) pay.
- **So the lab's three important problems are:**
  1. find new rents early;
  2. measure each one's decay from its first month;
  3. prove fast, because a slow proof spends the rent's best year.
- **A conclusion drawn from one window is a hypothesis,** until the longest series the lab holds says the same.
  Cycle 7's "pays in sell-offs" was reversed the next cycle by the 5.7-year panel.

## 5. Questions I never asked and should
- **Who exactly arbitrages the design gap today, and how big are they?** Open interest on the floorless legs and the
  history of funding gaps show how much is taken.
- **What is the owner's net after French tax and fees?** On a small stake it changes what is worth doing. I never
  computed it.
- **What does "breakthrough" mean for the owner, in euros and in time?** Their answer sets whether capacity or Sharpe
  is the target.
- **Where is the owner's small size an advantage?** Programs or venues with per-account caps, thin books that a fund
  cannot use, and retail-only products.
- **Can feedback be faster than forward paper?** Finer historical data (books, marks) answers some questions in
  hours. A tiny measured real pilot (rule 6, option B) answers costs in days.

## 6. How I will spend my time from now
- **Most of each cycle is reflection,** written:
  - the approach page above, applied to the subject at hand;
  - the mechanism map, filled from primary sources;
  - a short reflection on how I approached the last subject and what I would change.
- **A test, a tool or a measurement only when the reflection names it** as the cheapest decisive next observation.
- **Fewer rules, deeper ones.** I will not add a rule for every mistake. When a lesson recurs, I first ask which way of
  thinking produced the mistake, and change that.
