## 2. Fifteen improvements, ranked by expected impact

Each item: what changes; the mechanism; a proxy and target; the first step for the next cycle.

### 1. A decision gate in front of every freeze (the single biggest lever)
- **What changes.** Before a PREREG can be frozen, it must contain: the money decision each outcome triggers (stake,
  venue, size, or "nothing"); the break-even edge against cost; the power at the last look; the capacity (the smaller
  of the market's and the owner's, net of decay); the trial count N; and the pre-mortem's five lines. A script refuses
  to freeze a PREREG with an empty field.
- **Mechanism.** Four of five FAILs were visible before the freeze (self_review_20261123.md:19). Wide tests with no
  decision attached changed no money (0 of 2). A gate turns the lessons from prose into a condition for running.
- **Proxy and target.** Share of new rounds that freeze with all fields filled: 100% from the next round. Share of
  FAILs with a foreseeable cause named in the pre-mortem: at least 80%, so that the failures teach something new.
- **First step (next cycle).** Write `scripts/prefreeze_check.py` that reads a PREREG.md and checks the seven fields.
  Run it on the three open December PREREGs (R523, R527, R510) and list what they lack. Do not freeze new rounds
  without it.

### 2. Replay first: every idea gets a historical number in one day, forward lanes only after the replay passes
- **What changes.** For each idea, the first step is a replay on data the lab already holds (recorded books, funding,
  marks, the archive), reporting edge after cost, capacity in dollars, and decay by month. A forward lane needs a
  replay that passes.
- **Mechanism.** A forward lane takes 3-6 weeks; a replay takes minutes to a day once data is local (1.3). Replay turns
  a 6-week wait into a same-day answer and lets the lab screen many more ideas per week. L040 (the closed-market
  class, already recorded) and L036 (the rule before the data) show the replays that would have been cheap.
- **Proxy and target.** Median time from idea to first decisive number: from 3-6 weeks (forward) to under 1 day for
  ideas with local data. Ideas screened per week: from about 7 registrations to 50+ replays.
- **First step.** For the top three open ideas in methods.csv with data held (cost 1), write the replay in the same
  cycle and record its time in the registry notes.

### 2'. Haiku room with fixed roles and a verification protocol (the owner's request, made to work)
- **What changes.** Each cycle the lead sends six Haiku subagents the same fixed inputs (file paths and a single question
  each, with no conclusion). Roles:
  1. **Auditor:** hunts bugs and what could invalidate the lab's latest number (Feynman's hunt).
  2. **Red team:** writes how the idea loses money, from the registry's failures, before anyone argues for it.
  3. **Field reader:** reads one primary source in full and returns the equation or table that matters, with its page.
  4. **Base-rate clerk:** queries the registry and `scripts/base_rates.py` for the family's pass share, prior and N.
  5. **Venue-rule reader:** reads a venue's own rulebook, API field or docs for the mechanism.
  6. **Inventor:** proposes new mechanisms from the map's empty rows, each with a barrier and a payer.
- **Noise control.** Every claim cites path:line or a URL. The lead checks a fixed sample each cycle (for example 3
  claims per room member, chosen by the lead) and tracks accuracy by role. A role whose claims fail the check is
  rewritten or dropped. Disagreements are kept in the review file, not averaged away.
- **Mechanism.** Parallel reading and critique multiply the reading throughput, and the red team and auditor catch
  the failures that the lead's own favoured story hides. The lead still decides (CLAUDE.md:53).
- **Proxy and target.** Accuracy of room claims on the checked sample at 90% or better; the share of room claims that
  change a PREREG, a rule or a money number (target 1 in 3); ideas per cycle that reach a freeze.
- **First step.** Write the six role prompts as files in `docs/reviews/room_*/roles/` and run them once on the
  verdict-inflation question (1.6). Score the claims against the page before using any of them.

### 3. Verdict vocabulary that cannot inflate, and a deflated statistic for every PASS
- **What changes.** Split the verdicts: IN_SAMPLE_FIT, OOS_PASS (requires a holdout id), MEASUREMENT_PASS, NULL. A
  CONFIRMED without a holdout id is not allowed. Each PASS in FINDINGS reports N from the registry and a deflated
  Sharpe (Bailey et al.).
- **Mechanism.** Eight of thirteen CONFIRMED verdicts are one family (1.6). The label counts the same mechanism
  several times, so the lab believes it has more independent evidence than it has.
- **Proxy and target.** Number of economic passes that are OOS with an independent holdout: 0 now (count the holdouts
  you can name); target: every PASS has one. Share of PASS with deflated Sharpe reported: 100%.
- **First step.** Re-label the 13 CONFIRMED rows in a new column `verdict_v2` (not an overwrite; registry history stays)
  and count independent economic passes. Put the count in the December decision file.

### 4. A forecast ledger, scored automatically
- **What changes.** `registry/forecasts.csv`: one row per forecast (round, quantity, probability or number, date
  written, date due, outcome, Brier or absolute error). Every PREREG's forecast writes a row, and every decision date
  triggers a score.
- **Mechanism.** One scored forecast (R529) says nothing about calibration (1.5). Only a ledger, written before the
  result, gives the self-review a sample to score. A ledger also reveals whether the lab's prior (family pass share)
  is used at all.
- **Proxy and target.** Scored forecasts: 1 now; target 30 in 90 days. Brier score against the base rate: better than
  the base rate by month 3, or the prior is changed.
- **First step.** Create the file with the five existing forecasts (roll_frontrun_v1, xyzbal_fwd, tlteom_v1,
  rebalfront_v1, R529) and set the December due dates from the lane files.

### 5. A venue design card in one hour (the reusable tool)
- **What changes.** A harness that takes a venue name and its public API and writes one card: the funding formula
  parameters (interval, clamp, floor, dead zone), the mark and index construction, listing and delisting rules, 24 h
  volume per market, margin mode per market, the oracle source, and the sign of the funding gap by year. The card is
  the cheapest decisive observation the approach page asks for (approach_20261124.md section 3, step 6).
- **Mechanism.** L033 shows the venue's own field corrected a summary and saved a registered round. L026 shows the
  field name lies. A card made once per venue removes the hour of manual reading and the field-name errors, and it
  makes the census of venue rules (reflection_20261125_census.md) a repeatable query.
- **Proxy and target.** Time from venue name to card: from a day to under one hour. Venues carded per month: target 5.
- **First step.** Write the card template as a registry file with eight required fields, and fill it for the one venue
  the census flagged as having no card yet.

### 6. A portfolio of standing bets, each with a decay monitor
- **What changes.** The lab keeps 5-8 standing bets, each with a measured decay, a capacity, a correlation to the
  others, and a kill rule. New rents are searched in the places where demand is new (approach_20261124.md section 4d).
- **Mechanism.** The design gap decays about 30% a year (R525, 5.7 years). Its books are nearly uncorrelated (Sharpe
  8.5 for equal weight, CLAUDE.md "Change the scale"). A portfolio of decaying bets compounds only if new bets arrive
  faster than old ones decay: that is the pipeline, not a single edge.
- **Proxy and target.** Live bets with measured decay: now about 2 (Kraken funding, weekend fade); target 5 by spring
  2027. New uncorrelated rent found per quarter: target 1.
- **First step.** Make a one-table file of the standing bets: decay per year, capacity, correlation to the others, and
  the next scoring date.

### 7. An idea tournament scored by expected value per week of evidence
- **What changes.** Each cycle the room's inventors put ten candidate mechanisms through one score:
  EV = P(real) × min(market capacity, owner capacity) × access × (1 − decay) ÷ weeks to decisive evidence. The lead
  tests the top one and parks the next two.
- **Mechanism.** The lab chooses by lens and by what finished in two hours (approach section 2, item 1). The score
  makes the choice explicit and comparable across families, and it stops the cheap-but-small choices that dominate
  the list (1.2).
- **Proxy and target.** Rank correlation between score and outcome, measured over 20 choices (target above 0.4 by
  mid-2027). Money-changing tests per 10 tests: from 5 of 9 cycles to 7 of 10.
- **First step.** Write the score in one line in each PREREG's pre-mortem section and score the three open ideas
  before the next freeze.

### 8. A data-blocked list with an owner-yes queue
- **What changes.** The 89 BLOCKED methods that lack data get a single table: the data, its cost, its provenance, and
  whether the owner's yes is needed. Cheap downloads are batched; costly ones wait for the owner's yes.
- **Mechanism.** A third of the idea list is data-blocked (1.2). Fetching the one dataset that unlocks several ideas
  multiplies supply at once, which is how the 2 Po. question (room note of 2026-11-17) should have been answered.
- **Proxy and target.** Ideas unlocked per dataset fetched; blocked methods reduced from 120 to under 60 by the
  next quarter.
- **First step.** Sort the 120 BLOCKED rows by the data they name, and find the single dataset that unlocks the most.

### 9. Scheduled reads for the open rounds (never wait)
- **What changes.** Each frozen lane gets a scheduled task that runs its frozen scoring on the due date and writes the
  result into the decision file, so the lead reads it the same day.
- **Mechanism.** 100 open rounds and several December dates (1.3) would otherwise wait on the lead's attention. Rule 1
  ("never wait") is about the lead's time; scheduled scoring removes the waiting from it.
- **Proxy and target.** Days between due date and reading: target 0 to 1.
- **First step.** Schedule the December reads (R523 about 12-13, R527 12-15 and 12-25, R510 12-20, R521 12-22), each
  with its frozen scoring command.

### 10. A checklist of mistakes made into gates
- **What changes.** Turn the recurring mistakes in 1.7 into one-line checks that run before a freeze, a stake or a
  claim to the owner: sign of the book (short or long, measured), resolution of the data (hourly before any margin
  claim), first size times 2-10 before the owner sees it, venue field read against the venue's own page, plumbing
  checks on a seen weekend (the L042 double check).
- **Mechanism.** The same classes recur (1.7). A check run by a script catches a repeat without relying on memory or
  on luck.
- **Proxy and target.** Repeat occurrences of a catalogued class: from several per month to 0; new classes added per
  month: at least 1 (the list is alive).
- **First step.** Put the list in `scripts/prefreeze_check.py` (item 1) as warnings, not refusals, for one month.

### 11. A ledger of claims made to the owner, with the later value and a multiplier
- **What changes.** Each number told to the owner gets a row: claim, date, the binding constraint named, and the later

- **Mechanism.** Claims were optimistic every time (self_review_20261125 claims table). A ledger makes the optimism

- **Proxy and target.** Claim-to-later-value ratio within ×1.5 for claims older than one month; zero reversals not

### 12. Knowledge that answers "have we tested X?" in two minutes
- **What changes.** Mechanism cards (one per row of mechanism_map.md) generated from the registry and lessons: rounds

### 13. Lessons that change the gate, not only the story

### 14. A reading ledger with yield measured in numbers

- **First step.** Add the decision-table row to the close of each cycle (chain step 5) and count it for ten cycles

## 3. The three changes I would make first

1. **The freeze gate (item 1), with the decision table, the break-even, the power, the trial count and the forecast
   required fields.** It takes one script and one afternoon. It converts the most repeated failure (4 of 5 FAILs)
   into a condition, and it makes every later measurement comparable.
2. **Replay first (item 2) and the verdict vocabulary (item 3), together.** Replays make the lab's decisive number
   available in a day instead of 3-6 weeks, and the verdict split stops CONFIRMED from counting one family 8 times.
   Together they fix what is measured and how fast.
3. **The room with fixed roles and a sample check (item 2'), starting on the verdict-inflation question.** The owner has
   asked for it (CLAUDE.md:22, :53), and the sample check keeps the lead's judgment in the loop. Its first job is a
   result that can be checked against the page.

## 4. What "100x" can realistically mean here

Be honest about the arithmetic. The first stake is worth about €65 a year at the autumn level (L048). The owner's
target is €10,000 a year above cash (answers_20261124). A 100x on money therefore means ~€6,500 a year on the same
inputs, and that comes from capacity and several uncorrelated rents, not from faster testing alone. The census found
no new floorless venue (reflection_20261125_census.md), and the design gap decays about 30% a year (R525). So money at
100x needs the pipeline (item 6): new rents found in weeks, each measured for decay from its first month.

What is realistic for research speed:
- **Time to a decisive historical number: about 20-40x faster** (from 3-6 weeks to a day or less, for ideas with data
  held). This is the largest measured gap in the record (1.3).
- **Ideas screened per week: about 100x** (7 registrations to 50+ replays), but only if the data is held, and about a
  third of the idea list is not (1.2).
- **Forward decisions: not faster.** A forward lane still needs its calendar; the gain there is in scheduling and in
  choosing lanes whose power at the last look is above 50% (CLAUDE.md step 4).
- **Calibration: measurable in months, not weeks.** A ledger of 30 scored forecasts takes about 90 days at the current
  rate.
