# Leverage and margin for a Deribit + Hyperliquid design-gap stake, 2026-11-23

- **What this is.** Money readiness for Bet 1 (`docs/reviews/breakthrough_20261121.md`, item 4), ahead of R521's first
  look (about 2026-12-22).
- **It decides nothing.** A stake still needs a PASS and `lab.gate.go_real()`, placed by the owner.

## Tool
- `lab/margin.py` (tests in `tests/test_margin.py`):
  - `liq_move`: the uniform adverse move that liquidates one venue's cross-margin account;
  - `split`: the equity split between venues that equalises both moves;
  - `rebalance`: the transfer that restores a target distance.
- **Maintenance rates assumed:**
  - Hyperliquid at half its initial margin at max leverage: BTC 1.25%, ETH 2%, 10x alts 5%;
  - Deribit's rates were not read, so they are assumed the same.

## Stress, measured (`scripts/stress_dhl.py`, Deribit hourly closes, 2023-06-01..2026-11-19)
- **The basket:** an equal-weight basket of 15 liquid coins (BTC, ETH, SOL, XRP, AVAX, ADA, DOT, ALGO, BCH, DOGE, LINK,
  LTC, NEAR, TRX, UNI).
  - Worst 24 h: −24.2% / +20.9%. Worst 72 h: −28.9% / +33.5%.
- **Single coins:** worst 24 h down to −40% (ALGO), up to +86% (TRX).
  - With 20 slots on cross margin, one coin is about 5% of a venue's notional. A single-coin spike moves the account
    by a few percent; a market-wide move is what liquidates.

| Leverage (total notional / capital) | Move to liquidation, BTC-like | 10x alts |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 99% | 95% |
| 2 | 49% | 45% |
| 3 | 32% | 28% |
| 4 | 24% | 20% |
| 5 | 19% | 15% |

## Rule proposed for a first stake
- **Leverage at most 2.5** (each leg's notional 1.25x capital), with capital split by `margin.split`.
  - Distance to liquidation is then about 35%, about 1.5x the worst 24 h basket move.
  - That holds with rebalancing once a day.
- **Alerts and actions:**
  - rebalance when either venue's distance falls under 30% (`margin.rebalance` to 35%);
  - cut both legs pro rata if a transfer cannot restore 25%.
- **What it would earn.** R519's reported book was +4.5%/yr at leverage 1, so about +11%/yr at 2.5, before
  Hyperliquid's idle-USDC yield. That is an in-sample figure, which R520/R521 must confirm.

## Not checked
- Deribit's maintenance schedule, which may be tiered by size.
- Liquidation during an exchange outage or an oracle gap.
- Transfer delays between venues. A rebalance needs a withdrawal and a deposit, so the daily cadence assumes both
  clear within hours.

## Added 2026-11-23 after R525's audit: the tail is a broken hedge
- **The event.** The one-day-delay audit found LUNA (May 2022): one hedged trade lost about 34x a leg's notional
  before a daily check could close it.
- **Before any stake:**
  - an hourly ratio-divergence stop per pair (close both legs above about 10% from entry);
  - a cap of about 5% of book capital per coin;
  - no entry on coins with a venue halt, delisting notice or dead series;
  - minimum printing activity on both venues.
- These limits sit on top of the leverage cap above.

## Priced 2026-11-23 (R526, `experiments/risk_limits_v1/FINDINGS.md`)
- **Entry filters do the work.**
  - Price agreement at entry: with the one-day delay, it lifts the 12-pair portfolio from +4.6 to +7.2%/yr, and its
    worst day from −14.3% to −0.4%. At the base timing it costs nothing.
  - Printing activity on both legs: on the 4 hourly pairs, Sharpe 3.4 → 4.6 and max drawdown −2.8% → −0.7%.
- **The hourly stop on last-trade prices is dropped.** 92% of Bitfinex stops reverted within a day, and some
  "filled" at outlier prints. Max drawdown doubled.
- **Live form:**
  - agreement on book mids, normalized by the legs' usual ratio (for 1000x contracts);
  - a two-sided book with depth on both legs;
  - the engine's daily 20% blowout and dead-leg exits.

## The ticket, live (2026-11-23, `scripts/stake_ticket.py`, a dry run on public books)
- **What it does.** It turns the three forward lanes' open pairs into the owner's order list, venue by venue:
  - quantities rounded to each venue's lot;
  - R526's filters on live mids and depth;
  - each venue's initial and maintenance margin, from its public schedule where one exists;
  - deposits split so that both accounts are the same move from liquidation.
  It places nothing, and it reads no lane result.
- **Measured at $2,000 over three lanes** (about $42 a leg at 2.5x):
  - **Kraken's retail schedule binds.** Its public API gives retail clients 50% initial and 25% maintenance on
    most alts (2x); only the majors get 10x. At 2.5x the Kraken account must hold its full initial margin ($358),
    and the Hyperliquid account is then 35% from liquidation, near the 30% rebalance line.
  - **At 2x**, every account is 64-67% from liquidation (Bitfinex/Binance 160%).
  - **Minimums bite at small size.** At $42 a leg, Binance's minimums exclude BTC and ZEC, and lot steps leave one
    Deribit/Hyperliquid hedge 7% off. At $6,000 none is excluded.
  - **Books:** 2 of 20 Kraken books were one-sided tonight (PNUT, PEOPLE), so those pairs were skipped.
- **Access check.** Kraken's EEA contract list (support page "Perpetual contract specifications for clients in the
  EEA", updated 2026-06-29, 323 contracts) includes every coin R509 holds now, at "Class A-E (10x)" professional
  leverage. The retail limit is not on that page; the API's retail schedule above is the conservative reading.
- **Rule for the first stake (replaces 2.5):**
  - leverage at most 2.0;
  - $2,000 over the lanes that passed, or over two lanes rather than three if legs fall under venue minimums;
  - the ticket is run on the day, and every SKIP is honoured.

## How much of the owner's money: Thorp's rule with venue failure (2026-11-24, `scripts/stake_kelly.py`)
- **Variance does not bind.** On variance alone Kelly gives absurd leverage, about 390x at Sharpe 5.5. The binding
  risk is a jump: a venue failing and keeping its deposit (Thorp, section 7.3).
- **The model.** Growth = cash + stake × (L·m − cash) − variance + Σ over venues of λ·ln(1 − stake × deposit share ×
  (1 − recovery)).
  - The deposit shares come from the leverage-2 dry-run ticket: Hyperliquid 38%, Binance 18%, Bitfinex 16%, Kraken
    15%, Deribit 14%.
  - A small stake adds growth only if venues fail less often than **λ* = (L·m − cash)/(1 − recovery)** a year.
- **On the three forward pairs** (in sample +6.67%/yr, volatility 1.99%, cash 4.13%):

  | Edge kept forward | Leverage | Break-even failure rate (no recovery / half recovered) |
  |---|---|---|
  | all of it | 1 | 2.5% / 5.1% |
  | all of it | 2 | 9.2% / 18.4% |
  | **half (Thorp's rule)** | **2** | **2.5% / 5.1%** |
  | half | 1 | never above cash |

- **What it means:**
  1. Unlevered, a halved edge does not beat cash. Leverage 2 is needed, not optional.
  2. With a halved edge, the stake pays only if the venues used fail less than about 2.5-5% a year. That is close to
     plausible rates for crypto venues, so venue choice and spread matter as much as the edge.
  3. **Hyperliquid holds 38% of the deposits,** because R509 and R520 both short on it. R525 shows Deribit pays as
     much against Gate or Binance (+7.0 / +6.8%/yr against +4.4 with Hyperliquid). Moving the Deribit sleeve's
     short leg there would spread the venue risk. That move is untested forward: the forward evidence is on
     Deribit + Hyperliquid.
- **The rule after a PASS:**
  - Re-estimate m from the passing read's forward fund_book.
  - Run `python scripts/stake_kelly.py --m <forward m> --L 2 --lam <the owner's venue risk>`.
  - Stake at most half the growth-optimal fraction of the money the owner can lose, and never more than $2,000 on
    the first stake.

## Two accounts, not one pool (2026-11-24, `scripts/rebalance_sim.py`)
- **Why.** The engine nets both legs in one capital pool. A real book has one cross-margin account per venue. A rally
  drains the short venue and fills the long one, so collateral must move between them.
- **Method.** R525's in-sample books were replayed as two accounts.
  - Each leg is revalued at the daily close, with funding and 7 bp costs booked on the leg's own venue.
  - Maintenance: Kraken 25% (its most conservative retail schedule), Hyperliquid 10% (thin alts), others 5%.
  - At the daily check, if an account is under 30% from liquidation, transfer collateral so both accounts are equal.
- **Validation.** The two accounts' daily total equals the engine's book PnL to 10 decimals (unit test).
- **A flaw in my own margin model, found by this run.**
  - `lab/margin.py`'s uniform move nets an account's long and short legs. For a mixed account (Hyperliquid holds
    both directions in R509) it reads near-infinite, so the equalising split left that account at its bare
    maintenance.
  - The ticket and this simulation now use the move against every leg at once, (equity − Σ|N|·mm)/(Σ|N|·(1 + mm)).
    It is conservative and well posed.
  - **The ticket figures of 2026-11-23 (64-67% at 2x) used the flawed measure.** Corrected, at 2x: Kraken lane 29%,
    Deribit 42%, Bitfinex 154%. At 1.5x: 41%, 62% and 249%.
- **Results** (in sample, daily checks):

  | Pair | Transfers a year (1.5x / 2x) | Worst distance at a check (1.5x / 2x) |
  |---|---|---|
  | kraken + binance / gate / hyperliquid / kucoin | 1.2 / 11-47 | 30% / 20-26% |
  | same, Kraken maintenance 5% (EU entity's retail) | — / 1.2 | — / 28-30% |
  | bitfinex + binance / gate / kucoin | 1.6-2.3 / 3.5-4.9 | 24-27% / 9-18% |
  | bitfinex + hyperliquid | 13 / 33 | 21% / 13% |
  | deribit + binance / gate / hyperliquid / kucoin | 0-0.4 / 0.2-0.9 | 24-35% / 24-28% |

  - No account reached liquidation at any daily check, at either leverage.
  - Transfers are small when frequent (median 1-11% of capital on the Kraken pairs at 2x) and 17-37% of capital
    when rare.
- **Rule for the first stake (replaces "at most 2.0"):**
  - Kraken lane at 1.5x. It goes to 2x only if Kraken confirms in writing a 5% maintenance margin for an EU retail
    client on these coins.
  - Deribit and Bitfinex lanes at 2x.
  - Check once a day, and rebalance under 30%.
- **Not measured:**
  - intraday moves (Kraken, Hyperliquid and KuCoin are daily bars here);
  - the time a transfer takes;
  - single-coin spikes beyond the all-legs-against measure;
  - exchange outages.
