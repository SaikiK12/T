# Crypto strategy evidence review: carry, events, order flow, execution and access

**Research cut-off:** 2026-11-06. **Audience:** a small France-based crypto quant lab with USD 10k–100k, public APIs, seconds-level latency, and no colocation. This is a research report, not a live-trading recommendation. All sources were accessed on 2026-11-06 unless an entry says otherwise. External claims are kept separate from this repository's results.

## Scope and evidence rules

I searched primary exchange and regulator documentation, academic papers and preprints, public research, provider datasets, and the lab's native experiment records. “Net” means only the costs explicitly included by the cited test; a fee haircut is not a fill simulation. Backtests are not live records. A model that predicts a price move is not evidence of an executable strategy unless it also survives fill probability, fee tier, spread, funding, slippage, margin and liquidation.

Grades apply to the specific claim: **A** audited/live or peer-reviewed out-of-sample evidence with realistic costs; **B** credible out-of-sample evidence, with gaps disclosed; **C** historical backtest; **D** marketing, anecdote, synthetic result, or unsupported claim. A research result may receive B for predictability and D for net PnL.

The lab's own current records already contain more relevant evidence than several external marketing studies. I do not reopen the already-tested 5,472 chart rules, multi-asset momentum, regime/leader switching, short-straddle variance premium, Binance ML ranks, listing fades, panic buys or support/resistance classifiers without strong new out-of-sample evidence. The registry marks the current blind strategy-switching test R414 null as well. [R7] The strongest local carry results remain gate-0 historical estimates with incomplete execution and margin models; their forward lanes are still pending. The Boros instrument audit is a measurement, not strategy PnL. Current prospective records should remain frozen until their preregistered stopping rules are met. I found no sufficiently sourced, cost-inclusive 2023–2026 out-of-sample evidence to rank index-inclusion or buyback trades as actionable edges; the event section records the stronger available listing, delisting, unlock, airdrop and launchpool evidence. See the native files linked in [the source inventory](#sources-and-access-status).

## Executive summary: ten actionable findings, ranked

The ranking combines plausible net return with evidence quality and direct fit to the lab. “Actionable” means a next research decision, not a claim of proven alpha.

1. **Keep diversified cross-venue funding carry as the lead, but treat it as a small, unlevered paper candidate.** Local KuCoin/Gate and KuCoin/Binance books show roughly 10–12% annualized at 1x in historical data after fixed assumed costs, while the independent Hyperliquid/Binance/Bybit study shows a large regime decline to about 2% in 2026. None establishes current executable capacity or survival through exchange-specific margin events. **Grade C; forward proof pending.** [R1–R3; L9]
2. **Prioritize a realistic fill-and-margin replay of carry, not a larger parameter search.** The local models omit order-book fills, cross-exchange collateral, and liquidation; one stale-leg delisting error caused losses as large as 140% of a leg. The $10k–$100k capacity is not measured. [R1–R3]
3. **Treat the 10 October 2025 cascade as a mandatory paired-position stress test.** Binance reported transfer slowdowns and index dislocations; Hyperliquid ADL can close the profitable hedge leg; a paper reconstruction found negative funding/basis on SOL and DOGE on the event day. Do not extrapolate quiet-period drawdowns to this scenario. [L9; L15–L17; R2]
4. **Do not leverage carry from the headline APR.** BIS modeling finds a 10x futures leg would have been liquidated in more than half of months in its sample. Ethena's controls, off-exchange settlement, reserves and professional operations are not retail-replicable margin. Start at 1x and keep collateral on each venue. [L13–L14; L18–L20]
5. **Do not declare unlock shorts validated.** Local price-only results are attractive but use a revised, non-point-in-time calendar, omit exact fills and leave 14 funding observations unknown, including the worst price loss. Independent post-2024 research conflicts: one vendor reports a large pre-unlock median decline while a recent placebo study finds roughly 2% underperformance within noise. R266 is already the appropriate forward test. [R4–R5; R7; L23–L24]
6. **Use delisting and monitoring-tag events only as existing, low-sample forward candidates.** The lab's futures-delisting notice short was null at +1 hour, while an exploratory spot-notice and +24-hour tag variant looked better but has small samples and has not passed prospective gates. The lanes already exist; do not duplicate them. [R7]
7. **Order flow is more convincing as an execution/risk filter than as an alpha book.** A quarter-hour predictor had about 0.5 bp gross per trade against at least 4 bp maker round-trip cost; Hyperliquid wallet features improve short-horizon prediction in a retrospective split, but the authors explicitly do not establish executable profit. [L31–L36; R8]
8. **Airdrop/launchpool evidence is interesting but not yet an accessible short strategy.** The launchpool result is an abstract-only thesis with marginal significance; on-chain rapid selling is not equivalent to a borrowable, costed short. [L22; L30]
9. **Boros locks need current cost and settlement proof before capital allocation.** The lab's historical maturity audit found the best locked-vs-bill average was only 2.07 percentage points before an indicative 0.30% round trip; after that, the average falls below bills. Four live-paper locks are already being tracked. [R6]
10. **France venue eligibility is a binding research constraint.** OKX's EEA X-Perps is the clearest authorized derivative product found, but it is a fixed-expiry product rather than an ordinary perpetual. Several high-carry venues are either not authorized on the located record, explicitly exclude France, or have uncertain legal status. Legal access must be tested before a strategy is called deployable. [G1–G12]

## Findings

### 1. Cross-venue funding arbitrage: historical edge is real, current net edge and capacity are unproven

**Claim and exact evidence.** The best direct external study is Tony Lau's reproducible 2026 panel of BTC, ETH, SOL and DOGE funding on Hyperliquid, Binance and Bybit. It covers 1,133 aligned UTC days from 2023-05-12 through 2026-06-17. Its headline quote is: “Hyperliquid’s funding structurally exceeds Binance’s and Bybit’s on the same assets by roughly 7% annualized.” The annual regime figures and per-asset APRs below are transcribed from the package results tables. The annual average fell from 14.7% in 2024 to 4.6% in 2025 and about 2.0% in 2026 year-to-date; the latter has a HAC t-statistic of 4.7. Static, same-asset long/short portfolios report basis-adjusted APRs of 7.13% BTC, 7.07% ETH, 7.49% SOL and 9.27% DOGE for short Hyperliquid/long Binance. Reported maximum drawdowns are −1.89%, −1.15%, −3.63% and −11.28%, respectively. For short Hyperliquid/long Bybit, basis-adjusted APRs are 6.73%, 6.96%, 5.97% and 8.07%; drawdowns are −2.00%, −1.27%, −4.73% and −11.17%. These are published historical outputs, not live account returns. Lau reports APR on notional, whereas R275 reports return on total capital with both venue accounts funded; the headline rates are therefore not directly comparable without normalizing the capital base. [L9; R1]

**Local comparison.** The lab's R275 search evaluated 560 carry books over venue histories through 2026-09, with 7/14/21 bp per leg per side modeled costs. Its best Gate/Hyperliquid book reported +9.73% annualized at 1x, +8.51% at 2x and +7.28% at 3x, with −2.1% maximum drawdown; the best Binance/KuCoin replication in R277 reported +10.80%, +9.36% and +7.92% at 1x/2x/3x. R277's Gate/KuCoin result was +11.76% at 1x. A six-pair Lighter replication reported +6.20% at 1x for MEXC/Lighter under its lower cost assumption, and +5.88% when repriced at R275's higher cost levels. The blind long-tail R391 read reported +2.48%/+1.93%/+1.38% at 1x/2x/3x, but its top 10 days supplied 95% of the sum and costs were not measured. These are historical results, not a prospective pass. [R1–R3; R7]

**What the costs mean.** Lau's static portfolio charges a one-time 12 bp round trip over roughly three years, with no order-book fills, live tier-specific fees, transfer/remargining cost, or book-capacity check. Its dynamic sign-flip version changes state about 52 times/year; for BTC/ETH the estimated APR falls to 4.5%, 1.9%, −3.3% and −13.7% under 5/10/20/40 bp per-flip round-trip sensitivity. The local R275 cost assumptions are more conservative but still fixed deductions rather than realized executions. R275's audit found that 96–105% of measured gross was funding, but its book lacked intraday fills and liquidation replay. [R1–R3]

**Large published returns that do not qualify as net evidence.** Petar Zhivkov's 2026 paper examines 35.7 million one-minute observations across 749 symbols and 26 venues during 2025-11-08 through 2025-11-15; it says “only 40% of top opportunities generate positive returns after transaction costs and spread reversals,” and 95% of its modeled opportunities require a forced exit. The article says: “only 40% of top opportunities generate positive returns after transaction costs and spread reversals.” The article was only partially accessible, and snapshots are not independent trades; grade C for simulated spread behavior and D for implementable PnL. Edson Pindza reports 100.11% annualized return, Sharpe 38.82 and 2.45% maximum drawdown from a synthetic DEX-funding simulation on 2021–2024 data, with a $100,000 notional baseline, 3x leverage, 5 bp slippage per transaction and $50 gas per trade. The paper states: “The annualized mean return to the simulated funding rate arbitrage strategy is approximately 100.11%.” The DEX funding series is constructed, and the liquidation model omits venue maintenance tiers, cross-margin offsets and partial liquidation; this is **grade D for PnL**, not an implementable result. [L11–L12]

**Stress and counterevidence.** The external study's one-time cost haircut and the local carry models cannot establish a drawdown bound. On 2025-10-10, my reproduction from Lau's public archive of daily funding and a coarse basis proxy gives BTC +6.0 bp, ETH +2.3 bp, SOL −27.2 bp, DOGE −8.9 bp per notional; SOL reversed +27.5 bp the next day. This is an analyst recomputation, not the paper's reported event PnL. Binance describes a 33-minute transfer-system slowdown and index deviations during the broader cascade. Hyperliquid's ADL rules can close one side of a hedge. R277's dYdX sets also demonstrate that stale crash marks and constant-notional accounting can generate implausible losses and rebounds. [L9; L15–L17; R2]

**Grade, capacity and fit.** **C.** The funding differential is reproduced, but executable net returns and current capacity are unverified. The local R275 K20 sizing implies $250 per leg at $10,000 equity if both venue accounts are funded equally; at $100,000 the same rule implies $2,500 per leg. Those are arithmetic allocations, not observed market capacity. Depth, concurrent open-interest impact and margin liquidity were not measured. Carry is also legally constrained: KuCoin global futures, MEXC, Binance futures, Gate and Hyperliquid do not all have clearly eligible French retail access. [R1–R3; G1–G12]

**Exact lab verification.** Keep existing R278/R279/R294/R390/R391/R285 lanes frozen. For any next decision, build one execution replay from timestamped public trades and order books: synchronized two-leg entry/exit, actual fee schedule by account tier, queue/fill probability for maker orders, taker book sweep for each notional, per-market funding settlement and interval, venue-specific isolated/cross margin, collateral haircuts, and one-leg liquidation/ADL. Report by asset, venue, side, $10k/$25k/$50k/$100k notional, and stressed transfer outage. Do not open another broad carry grid before these missing mechanics are measured. [R1–R3]

### 2. Risk sizing, leverage and what professional delta-neutral books actually imply

**Claim.** A nominally market-neutral pair is exposed to two separate margin engines, funding reversal, basis divergence, exchange credit risk, collateral transfer delays and forced closure of only one leg. BIS Working Paper 1087 models a BTC cash-and-carry trade on CME futures from 2019–2024. Its warning is exact: “assuming a leverage of 10 … the futures leg … would have been liquidated in over half of the months in our sample.” The paper reports an 87% maximum drawdown for that isolated futures leg in its modeled history; this is not a combined portfolio drawdown or a live strategy result. **Grade B for risk evidence, not for a tradable return.** [L13]

ANB Investments described keeping minimum exchange margin at 15–20% during counterparty concerns and around 30% margin/equity in a large quant strategy. The manager reported less than 1% maximum drawdown, but no audited public return series was provided; it also said the incremental delta-neutral premium after FTX was not worth the counterparty risk. This is an unaudited operator anecdote, **grade D**. [L14]

Ethena is useful as an operational case study, not a retail sizing recipe. Its own Q3 2026 committee update states: “The Reserve Fund balance was $62.57M against a recommended Reserve Fund size of $45.7M, a multiple of 1.4 times.” It reported a $62.57m reserve against a $45.7m recommended amount, 1.4x coverage; its reported 7-day sUSDe APY at quarter-end was 5%. A separate governance update reported 8.2% mean APY over 2024-09-01–2026-02-13, with 6.6% in the Jan 2025–Feb 2026 segment. These are issuer/committee disclosures and sUSDe yield includes more than perp funding. A February 2025 Chaos Labs/Aave analysis of the Bybit hack reported over $1bn of Ethena hedge exposure and under $30m of unsettled PnL; it notes: “balances are settled daily, meaning that Ethena maintains risk exposure to the daily profits/losses from its short positions.” This is an independent secondary event analysis, not an audited return record. [L21] Ethena reported an average sUSDe APY of 16.7% in Q4 2024 and a 5% 7-day APY at Q3 2026 quarter-end; it also reported $0.84bn BTC/ETH hedges (17% of backing), with Binance at 12%, OKX 4% and Bybit 2%. These issuer figures are not a pure perp carry return. The issuer wrote: “Approximately $1.9 billion in USDe redemptions were processed between the 10th and the 11th of October.” Its report also contains a $1.2bn summary figure; the analysis is not an independent audit. Ethena's proof-of-reserves provider warns: “No assurance is provided over the automated reporting itself.” Proof of reserves does not audit strategy PnL. [L39] [L18–L20]

**Exact lab verification.** Before any leverage test, replay each leg independently against current exchange risk tiers, mark/index behavior, liquidation fee, insurance-fund/ADL logic and symbol limits. Show equity available on each venue after worst observed funding debit and stressed price gaps; do not assume collateral can be transferred during a cascade. Use 1x notional per side for first forward evidence. A leverage multiple should be accepted only after an adverse-event replay and a forced-close test; never infer safety from a calm-period maximum drawdown.

**Counterparty and venue lifecycle.** I found no source-confirmed insolvency among the covered venues for 2024–2026. That is a bounded literature search, not evidence of low default risk. A 2025 peer-reviewed study finds spot Bitcoin cross-exchange premiums associated with default risk persist longer at riskier exchanges; it is supporting evidence for counterparty exposure, not a perp-carry return estimate. BitMEX ceased exchange operations on 2026-09-23; its official wind-down notice describes a closure, not an insolvency. [L10; L37]

### 3. Token unlock shorts: sizable historical charts, weak point-in-time and net evidence

**Claim and local evidence.** R265's historical short of insider/private-sale unlocks reported 422 priced events across 69 perps and mean returns of +3.24%, +3.04% and +2.84% per event at 1x/2x/3x, with six losses greater than 40% and RIVER at −132%. R258 independently repriced 421 of 530 events and reported +3.261%/+3.061%/+2.861% at 1x/2x/3x after fixed round-trip costs of 20/40/60 bp; R259's funding bounds covered only 407 of 421 priced events; 14 unknown funding cases included the cohort's worst price loss. It reports a conditional 1x mean of +2.625% when unknown cases are excluded, and some paths needed collateral above initial notional. These are not realized returns: the calendar was revised after the events, the tests are historical gate-0 (not OOS or live), exact fills and borrow availability are absent, and RIVER is an extreme counterexample. Capacity at $10k–$100k is not measured. The already-running R266 forward lane records point-in-time snapshots and measured paper costs; do not replace it with another historical calendar search. [R4–R5; R7]

**Independent results conflict.** Tokenomist's 2026 summary reports a 14.7% median decline in the month before an unlock for 164 events, with p<0.001, stating: “the one month pre-event median is -14.7% (p < 0.001, n=164)”; its broader matched sample shows a −4.85% effect over 236 events, while the mature-token estimate is −2.57% and insignificant versus −16.02% for new/early tokens. The author says token type alone is not decisive; float and unlock size relative to market cap matter. This is a vendor study with one market cycle and no borrow/funding/entry execution. Maza Research's 2026 placebo analysis says: “Before unlocks, tokens did about 2% worse than the placebo over 30 days.” Its >=5%-of-supply group looked closer to −6% but was unreliable; revised schedules erase the effect, and random-calendar placebo Sharpe was 0.26±0.40 compared with the strategy's 0.53 after modeled costs. Keyrock's 2024 research says: “90% of unlocks create negative price pressure, regardless of size or type.” It reports that negative pressure across 16,000 events for 40 tokens, with team unlocks around −25% and ecosystem unlocks +1.18% in its reported event buckets. The pressure is measured over an event window of about 30 days; overlapping windows and absent OOS/cost accounting make that a backtest claim. An EPFL thesis over 500 top tokens in 2019–2024 reports first-unlock CARs around −2% to −3% (with first events roughly 2–4% depending on event window) and states: “after the third unlock, the effect of a token unlock declines from 2–3% to between 0–1% and becomes statistically insignificant.” These sources differ in event definitions, survivorship, matching and cycle, so the headline 14.7% is not a forecast. [L23–L26]

**Grade, capacity and test.** **C for local historical price association; D for executable net returns.** No public capacity curve for $10k–$100k was found. Required test is already underway in R266: preserve first-seen event-calendar snapshots; lock event size relative to circulating float, investor/team/ecosystem type, and entry time before price read; use an independent matched control and random-date placebo; include short borrow availability, venue funding, liquidation margin, order-book depth and full costs. Report extreme positive squeezes and unknown calendar revisions rather than dropping them. [R4–R5; R7; L23–L26]

### 4. Listings, delistings, monitoring tags, airdrops and launchpools

**Delistings and tags.** Local R267's Binance futures-notice short at the first hourly open and +1h cover was null: −0.12% at 1x, t=−0.06, after a fixed 40 bp 1x round-trip cost and settled funding, with severe squeeze examples. The logged exploratory spot-notice +1h result was +4.15% (t=3.07, n=25), but only one of eight logged variants and not a pass. R269's monitoring-tag short was +0.61% at +1h (t=0.71) and +4.36% at +24h (t=3.81) after a 40 bp 1x round-trip cost and funding on a 24-cluster historical sample; its +24h mean fell from +8.45% in 2025 to +1.31% across 11 2026 clusters. Forward paper lanes R268 and R270 already test these variants. Neither study measures depth, borrow availability or $10k–$100k capacity; no event result is a prospective pass. [R7]

A 2026 academic Binance-delisting prediction paper uses 104 announcements from Jan 2023–Mar 2025 and reports accuracy .9528, precision .722, recall .762, F1 .729 and ROC AUC .937 under random stratified cross-validation, across only 34 unique delisted coins. The authors write: “approximately 79% (82 cases) announced just 2 days in advance.” This is notice classification, not strategy PnL or an independent temporal holdout; grade B for classification prediction, D for tradable returns. A dynamic vendor sample of 95 Binance spot delistings reports: “the median coin was -16.8% after 24 hours (-16.6% relative to BTC) and -32.7% after 7 days”; it has no borrow, fee or execution model (grade D for net returns); its capacity is unknown. Conversely, EdgeOrNot's 348 new Binance perpetual listings result is +0.61% mean after modeled fees/slippage/funding with t=0.24, but its first half was +2.28% and its second half −1.27%; the study reports “average +0.61% after costs and funding (t 0.24); on the second half of the data -1.27%.” That is evidence against a robust generic listing short; grade C for the historical cost-modeled backtest and D for live deployment, with no verified $10k–$100k capacity. [L27–L29]

**Airdrops and launchpools.** A full arXiv study covering six airdrops reports: “a substantial share of tokens--up to 66% in some cases--are rapidly sold, often in recipients' first post-claim transaction.” That is wallet behavior, not a short signal: claim eligibility, vesting, hedgeability, venue listing, borrow and timing matter. A 2026 NCCU thesis abstract reports +1.29% per launchpool event, marginally significant at 10%, stating that the strategy “yields a per-event return of +1.29%, marginally significant at the 10% level” in 929 events across four exchanges from 2020–2025; the full thesis is embargoed until 2031. Cross-staking is reported at +3.13%, while self-staking at −0.65% is insignificant. Treat as a lead only, **grade D** until the full design, OOS protocol, fees and tradable token path can be inspected. The abstract does not establish net costs or $10k–$100k capacity. [L22; L30]

**Exact lab verification.** Do not turn an event-study cumulative return into short PnL. Capture official announcement first-seen timestamps, token eligibility and product, and a simultaneously executable venue book. Compare event cohorts to matched non-events and randomized announcements. For delisting/tag lanes already running, wait for their preregistered event count and cost gates.

### 5. Order-flow and short-horizon prediction: useful information, little evidence of net alpha

**Quarter-hour effect.** Kim and Hansen's 2026 preprint studies Binance USD-M aggregated trades for BTC, ETH, XRP, SOL, DOGE and ADA from 2021-01 through 2024-10. It uses monthly walk-forward refits and reports an out-of-sample combined-model R² of 3.37%, AUC .6011 and 57.12% hit rate across roughly 117,000 asset-quarter-hour boundaries. The average gross return is about 0.5 bp/trade. The authors' cost comparison is explicit: “the average gross per trade of about 0.5 bps is roughly one-tenth of a single taker fee.” Even two 2 bp maker fills cost 4 bp before adverse selection, so the predictable effect is not an executable strategy at ordinary retail fees. **Grade B for predictability, D for net PnL; no capacity test.** [L31]

**Hyperliquid wallet identity.** A Nov 2026 preprint studies 17.1 billion Hyperliquid messages, 14.3 million orders and 147,113 wallets across 10 markets from 2026-07-01 through 2026-07-27, with BTC/ETH/SOL evaluation. Wallet identity raises one-second ridge R² from 10.88% to 12.31%; a tree model moves from 19.48% to 20.65%. At a 200/300 ms delay, the relative feature benefit remains, but this is a retrospective train/validation design and has no fees or executable portfolio. Its authors state: “These exercises … do not … demonstrate executable profit.” **Grade B for a prediction-feature lead; D for strategy PnL; no $10k–$100k capacity or trading-cost test.** [L32]

The 2026 SSRN study of rejected Hyperliquid post-only orders and the 2026 Journal of Financial Markets paper on spot order flow support the idea that flow can contain information, but neither directly validates this lab's altcoin-perp taker-imbalance book. A Bybit BTC/ETH toxicity study covers 360 walk-forward out-of-sample days from 2025-02 through 2026-02 and roughly 31 million seconds per asset; its TailScore is an adverse-selection risk rank, not a trading strategy. **Grade B for risk ranking, D for PnL.** [L35] A separate Hyperliquid study examines 4.3m hidden metaorders and 465k visible TWAPs for execution behavior, not directional returns. [L42] The latter uses about 82 spot assets from 2018 through June 2022 with expanding out-of-sample tests beginning in 2020. It reports: “the long-short portfolio for the best model (SGB) delivers a daily mean return of 0.76% and an annualized SR of 3.68.” Its stylized break-even cost is about 0.54% per day, not an observed fee schedule. This has expanding OOS but remains historical spot research, grade B for that research claim and D for transferring it to perps; no $10k–$100k perp capacity is established. [L34] The lab has already had an altcoin taker-flow holdout fail and local trade-size/count and options-flow tests are null; do not repeat basic imbalance ranks. [L33–L36; R8]

**Best next use.** Explore wallet/rejected-order signals as a fill-quality or risk gate only if the lab can acquire licensed raw order-level data. Pre-register a prospective scoring rule, latency-delay variants, fees, maker queue position and markout. Compare the signal against a no-signal execution policy. The threshold is positive realized net PnL at the actual route, not improved R².

### 6. Boros funding locks: reliable settlement mechanics do not imply excess return

R412 measured 67 mature BTC/ETH Boros markets and 3,250 daily entries over 2025-08–2026-09. It found oracle coverage of 99.96% on Binance and 99.70% on Hyperliquid, median absolute locked-versus-realized difference of 0 bp, and a 95th percentile of 0/3.6 bp APR; the worst observed difference was 54 bp APR in the 10 Oct crash hour. Mean locked rate minus T-bill was +0.08 percentage points for Binance and +2.07 points for Hyperliquid. An indicative 0.30% spot-plus-perp round trip erased the average excess over bills. This is an audit of the instrument's settlement, not a trading backtest. R413 already paper-tracks four live locks on $10k notional and waits for its preregistered settlement gate. [R6]

**Grade C for historical basis measurement; no grade for strategy returns yet.** The maturity comparison is in-sample, not a prospective return test. Capacity depends on current Boros order-book depth and the paired perp book; not found in the maturity audit. Continue the existing forward lane and record bid/ask, fee, hedge fill, settlement/oracle discrepancy, margin and exit cost. Compare net return to a T-bill after all legs, not to gross locked APY.

### 7. What the Oct 10 2025 liquidation cascade teaches about carry and hedges

Binance's Jan 2026 retrospective says its internal transfer subsystem slowed for about 33 minutes; the reported interval was 21:18–21:51 UTC. It also reports a separate index-price deviation window and says many liquidations preceded the USDe-related dislocation. This is an exchange account, so its market-wide causal interpretation is not independent. The event still demonstrates that “delta-neutral” can fail operationally when venues stop matching, collateral cannot move, or marks diverge. [L15]

Hyperliquid documents ADL that ranks profitable opposite-side positions and closes them at the prior mark when liquidations exhaust the backstop. A 2026 preprint reconstructs $2.1bn of positions ADL'd over 12 minutes and distinguishes PnL-dollar overshoot from a much larger equity-dollar diagnostic; the latter is not realized customer loss. A separate study of seven BTC cascades finds a cross-event order-flow-variance precursor, but labels it “a population-level precursor, not a per-event alarm”; it has no trading PnL. [L41] Ethena's issuer report says roughly $1.9bn of USDe redemptions were processed across the event, while other issuer sections quote $1.2bn; the report's own internal figures disagree and are not independent verification. [L16–L17; L19]

**Exact lab test.** For each candidate pair, simulate: one venue's mark/index gap; funding sign and cap changes; stale or missing settlement; transfer outage; collateral haircut; liquidation of only one leg; ADL on one venue; and forced close via order-book sweep. Preserve the loss before recovery in a separate line so constant-notional accounting cannot create artificial PnL after a position would have liquidated. Report own-market trades and independent incident facts separately.

### Slippage and paper-account implementation

For taker orders, sweep the timestamped order book at the target size for each leg, then add the actual route's taker fee and modeled decision-to-arrival delay. For maker orders, model queue position, partial fill, cancel/reprice and adverse post-fill markout; a touch at the mid or best quote is not a fill. A 2025 live-execution study on Bybit and Binance found profitable taker orders had a notable probability of failing to execute after intervening book updates. [L36]

The minimum durable cost ledger is: entry fee + entry slippage + exit fee + exit slippage + funding debits/credits + borrow (if any) + hedge drift while one leg is unfilled + collateral/transfer cost + liquidation/closeout cost. Report filled and unfilled opportunities separately. Calibrate paper fills against a live recorder; if raw historical order books are unavailable, prospective recording is the honest path. Do not plug a single “round-trip bp” into an event or carry system and call it net.

## Open questions and next verification gates

- **Net carry:** What are realized taker and maker costs for each France-eligible product at target size, and how much of the historical return remains after one-leg margin stress? Public studies do not answer this. Local forward lanes do not yet have enough days to decide.
- **Capacity:** No reliable $10k–$100k capacity curve was found for the candidate books. Need timestamped depth/queue and impact by pair, not 24-hour volume.
- **October cascade accounting:** Independent venue-level liquidation, ADL, mark-price and transfer logs are incomplete. Binance and Ethena reports are primary but interested-party accounts; the ADL paper contains model-dependent “avoidable” diagnostics.
- **Venue solvency:** No insolvency in the reviewed 2024–2026 venue sample was verified. Absence of a discovered event does not measure default probability; spot default-risk papers do not estimate a current perp pair's risk.
- **Unlock point-in-time history:** Existing lab snapshots begin in October 2026; older calendars were revised. Historical signal decay by unlock type cannot be established without archived first-seen versions.
- **Order book history:** Free broad CEX L2 history was not found. Hyperliquid and Lighter provide partial historical paths; paid vendors offer more, but sample validation and exact capture coverage remain necessary.
- **France derivatives authorization:** The legal status of self-custodial protocols and several offshore venues is uncertain. A venue not naming France in a blocklist is not a positive legal authorization. OKX EEA X-Perps is a different instrument from a standard perp.
- **Boros net yield:** Historical oracle agreement is strong in the measured sample, but the indicative spot/perp cost erased mean excess versus bills. R413 must settle before this can be called a yield strategy.
- **No “100% trade” exists:** A filled trade can still lose through mark movement, hedge mismatch, funding changes, liquidation, ADL, venue failure or settlement. The project can improve confidence by measuring these failure modes, not by assigning certainty to a chart signal.
