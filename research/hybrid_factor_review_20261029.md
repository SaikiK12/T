# Hybrid factor review: interactions and regime conditioning

Date: 2026-10-29  
Scope: bounded primary-source review of small combinations around R304/R305. No new project market-data outcomes were computed or read; external papers’ reported results were read as literature evidence. No data were downloaded, no tests were run, and no frozen file was changed.

## Decision

Do not open a new historical factor family now. R304's 2024–2026 window was already read by R299; R304 and R305 are gate-0 diagnostics, not fresh confirmation. The current panel reports 9,479,191 program trials and zero strict on-time paper days overall. R371 is a separate US-open momentum ticket, not an R304 confirmation. The current M042 path remains forward confirmation plus a missing-metrics audit; that should happen first.

The papers do make three bounded, falsifiable future variants worth preserving as hypotheses. If the M042 forward stream becomes operational and has sufficient on-time days, choose **one** of these before outcomes are read. Do not run all three and select a winner. The first has the strongest direct link to R304's flow/momentum inputs; the second is the cleanest regime hypothesis; the third is only a short-risk overlay. None is ready for historical re-testing on R304's seen period.

## Project evidence and overlap

- **R304 / M042.** The clean model is a weekly 7-day GBM ranker on 24 features: price and volume (including mom7/mom30, qvol30, taker7, tradesize7), funding/basis, spot, OI/positioning and age. On the already-read 2024-01–2026-09 window, its net was +58.9 / +56.8 / +54.6 bp/day at 1x / 2x / 3x, t=4.21, with 1.76 weekly turnover. R299 had already examined that window, so it is a historical lead only. See [R304 PREREG](../experiments/mlrank_v2/PREREG.md) and [R304 FINDINGS](../experiments/mlrank_v2/FINDINGS.md).
- **R305.** The score is not explained by a simple risk composite: risk-only was −15.7 bp/day and the low-risk composite −21.3, while a post-hoc no-risk GBM was +62.4. This is not fresh evidence. Short squeezes remain a material implementation risk: 15.5% of validation weeks had a short constituent up over 100%; the maximum was +1,850%. The long leg carried most of the historical alpha; the short leg's alpha was weak. [R305 FINDINGS](../experiments/mlaudit_v1/FINDINGS.md).
- **R238 / R302.** R238's one-way mom7 book was +9 bp/day at t=1.77 in discovery, then +2.4 in 2022 and +1.5 in 2023; validation was not run. R302's low-funding sort had +25.3 bp/day in discovery but −7.2, t=−0.71, in validation. Its old positive result came from price returns in 2021; the price leg reversed while the carry remained positive. [R238 FINDINGS](../experiments/xsbattery_v1/FINDINGS.md), [R302 FINDINGS](../experiments/fundxs_v1/FINDINGS.md).
- **Regime and combination overlap.** M003/R260 already tried a high-dispersion plus negative-alt-trend gate on older flow books and lost Sharpe. M158/R233 tested BTC trend × volatility regimes and a regime mix without a useful gate. M171/R238 tested funding slope and was flat. M042 already includes non-linear interactions among momentum, volume/taker, basis/funding and positioning. Parent additionally identified R187's merge, R228's 30 static flow mixtures and R309's 50/50 carry/spot mix as null. None of the candidates below is a new static sleeve allocation, but all overlap with tested ingredients and must be charged as additional trials.
- The frozen R304 features include OI and positioning with substantial archive missingness. R305 explicitly leaves missingness as a feature-driver audit. Avoid any model using those fields until that audit is resolved.

## Primary evidence and access limits

1. **Mercik, Będowska-Sójka, Karim & Zaremba (2025), “Cross-sectional interactions in cryptocurrency returns,” International Review of Financial Analysis 97, 103809.** The publisher abstract and indexed article preview report 40 characteristics, 9,360 double-sorted combinations and a 2017–2023 universe of 500+ coins/tokens. The strongest interactions involve liquidity, risk and past returns; reported selected OOS long/short Sharpe is 1.54 equal-weighted and 1.06 value-weighted. The preview says moderate costs can leave results positive, particularly on the long side, while higher costs hurt especially on shorts. It also says equal-weight portfolios show many more significant mean returns than value-weighted portfolios and that top equal-weight interactions are concentrated in liquidity, volatility and tail-risk characteristics. These are broad spot/token results, not a MEXC-perpetual implementation. The publisher preview was readable; the full article/author-manuscript PDF returned 403, so I could not verify sample selection, exact OOS split, exact cost levels, funding/borrow treatment or the full interaction tables. Treat it as an interaction hypothesis, not a recipe: [publisher article](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1057521924007415), [repository record and license](https://open.icm.edu.pl/handle/123456789/25545).

2. **Anastasopoulos et al. (2026), “Order flow and cryptocurrency returns,” Journal of Financial Markets 79, 101047, DOI 10.1016/j.finmar.2026.101047.** The final article is marked open access on the publisher page, but that page returned 403. I read the authors’ November 2024 51-page conference version, which uses 82 daily spot coins, 11 international-currency order-flow series aggregated across exchanges, and a recursive train/validation/test design (test: 2020-02–2022-06). It orthogonalizes lagged flow to lagged returns to separate flow information from short-term reversal; its nonlinear SGB forecasts beat its linear/fundamental comparators. It is an important mechanism precedent for flow incremental to returns, but its balanced panel requires end-sample market cap above $1m and continuous nonzero prices/volume across the period, creating survivorship risk; its signed multi-currency world flow is not Binance taker-buy ratio. Its 0.3–0.5% spot-cost references and daily break-even calculations are not comparable to this project’s perp/API costs. Final-journal findings may differ from this conference version; do not treat exact 2024-version numbers as final-journal claims. [Final journal record](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.finmar.2026.101047), [readable 2024 conference paper](https://www.efmaefm.org/0EFMAMEETINGS/EFMA%20ANNUAL%20MEETINGS/2025-Greece/papers/OrderFlowpaper.pdf).

3. **Chi, Hao, Hu & Ran (2023), “An empirical investigation on risk factors in cryptocurrency futures,” Journal of Futures Markets 43(8), 1161–1180.** The full open-access Wiley HTML was readable. It studies 12 large OKEx futures coins from 2017–2021, not a broad perpetual universe. Basis is the stronger factor; momentum is weak and holding-period-sensitive. Their constructed basis-momentum factor loses significance after controlling for basis, and its rankings correlate highly with basis. Daily results are stronger than weekly/monthly; the very short-horizon result is not directly transferable to R304's 7-day perp book. A 5 bp cost sensitivity is not an API/depth/funding model. This is a useful disconfirming result against treating basis × momentum as a new independent alpha family. [Full article](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/fut.22425), [Cambridge record](https://www.repository.cam.ac.uk/items/3a556482-574b-42cd-af07-fe5c9f9db91c).

4. **Hsieh, Huang & Liu (2025), “State transitions and momentum effect in cryptocurrency market,” Finance Research Letters 86, 108356.** The publisher/DOI preview reports weekly 2015–2023 data and defines a market state from the prior four-week value-weighted cryptocurrency return; momentum is strongest in UP→UP state transitions and absent in other transitions. Full text was inaccessible in this review, so universe construction, survivorship treatment, exact performance and costs are unverified. This is a precise regime hypothesis, but it is spot-market evidence and its period is historical. [Publisher DOI record](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.frl.2025.108356).

5. **Grobys et al. (2025), “Cryptocurrency momentum has (not) its moments,” Financial Markets and Portfolio Management 39, 443–476.** Full open-access article read. Its weekly top-30-by-prior-year-market-cap sample from 2016–2023 finds an overall insignificant momentum payoff and a −255% one-week crash driven by one short-leg coin rising about 1,400%. It attempts to recover delisted coins but excludes residual missing cases. Volatility management can improve average/risk-adjusted results in its setup, yet it does not significantly alter the power-law tail risk. This is a direct warning that a broad BTC-vol gate cannot remove the single-name squeeze risk visible in R305. [Full article](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11408-025-00474-9).

6. **Version distinction for the Cao/Zhai/Luo work.** The peer-reviewed 2024 *Journal of Futures Markets* record is the 134-predictor, 48-significant-return version, with a basis plus price-volume model; only its abstract/research record was accessible, and the author manuscript is embargoed. A distinct 2026 SSRN posting by Cao, Luo, Cheng and Dong reports 170 predictors and 63 significant total-return sorts; only the abstract was accessible. Do not attribute the newer 170/63 figures to the 2024 journal version or treat either abstract as verification of tradable PnL. Both suggest common basis/price-volume structure, which argues that many apparent predictors may overlap rather than represent independent alpha. [2024 journal record](https://www.research.ed.ac.uk/en/publications/anatomy-of-cryptocurrency-perpetual-futures-returns/), [2026 SSRN abstract](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=6795783).

7. **Zhang & Makgolo (2026), “Cross-Sectional Dispersion and the State Dependence of Cryptocurrency Momentum,” SSRN preprint.** Abstract-only; the PDF returned 403 and this is not peer reviewed. It claims that lagged cross-sectional dispersion, especially its upper tail, predicts weaker momentum after controlling for BTC volatility and correlation, using a survivorship-aware dynamic CoinGecko top-500 universe. This is a useful counterpoint to generic volatility gates, but it overlaps the project's R260 dispersion gate and R238 momentum work. I would not prioritize it over forward data. [SSRN abstract](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=6648082).

## Exact small variants worth retaining, not launching on the seen window

| Rank | Pre-specified rule | Controls and trial count if later registered | What makes it distinct; key disconfirmation |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **7-day momentum × residual taker-flow confirmation.** At each weekly decision, form the point-in-time top-150 MEXC-tradable Binance-perp universe by trailing 30-day volume. Cross-sectionally rank mom7, qvol30 and taker7. Regress the same-date taker7 rank on an intercept, mom7 rank and qvol30 rank; rank the residual. Equal-weight the top-left corner where both mom7 and residual-flow ranks are ≥80th percentile, and short the bottom-right corner where both are ≤20th percentile. Hold 7 days and rebalance weekly. No fitted return model or threshold search. | Three books: mom7-only top/bottom quintiles; residual-flow-only top/bottom quintiles; the joint corner portfolio (primary). Count all 3. The pre-registered primary contrast is joint net return minus the mom7-only book; the flow-only book is a secondary control. Adjust family-wise inference across all 3. | Order-flow research supports incremental flow after conditioning on returns, and Mercik et al. report that past-return × liquidity/risk interactions can matter. But R304's GBM already contains mom7, qvol30 and taker7, so a historical R304 re-fit is overlapping and invalid as confirmation. The published order-flow study uses international signed spot flow; taker7 is only a local proxy. R238's one-way momentum weakened after 2021. |
| 2 | **R304 score × persistent-UP regime.** Keep the frozen R304 h7 GBM scores, quintile sides, universe, 7-day holds and sizing unchanged. Trade the same book in all weeks as the baseline; the challenger takes the same positions only when the lagged market regime is UP→UP: state(w)=UP iff the point-in-time eligible-universe return over the prior four complete weeks is nonnegative, and require state(w)=state(w−1)=UP at the rebalance. When the gate is off, hold cash; do not redistribute the budget to other weeks. | Two books: frozen all-week R304 and UP→UP-gated R304. Primary estimand is the paired change in net weekly PnL on the same fresh dates, plus absolute net/cost results. Count both. | The 2025 state-transition article gives an explicit state variable different from a BTC volatility gate. R233/R260 already found generic regime filters unhelpful on the older flow books; R304 is a different nonlinear cross-section, but its seen period cannot answer this. The source is broad spot, not perp, and R304 earns in years when an UP→UP-only rule may discard profitable weeks. |
| 3 | **Funding-stress guard on R304 shorts (risk overlay, not a new return signal).** Preserve R304's long side. Before opening a short, omit any bottom-quintile name whose venue-specific trailing 3-day funding rate is below the expanding 5th percentile computed from that venue's prior 365 days. Keep omitted notional in cash; no reallocation and no new stop/cover optimization. Use MEXC funding for MEXC trades and record settlement cash separately. | Two books: frozen R304 baseline and the single funding-guarded variant. Count both. The primary comparison is short-tail loss and total net PnL, not a selected Sharpe. | R302 shows that the low-funding cross-section is not a stable price signal and flags squeezed negative-funding perps, while R305 documents extreme short squeezes. However R304's short leg historically ranked high, not low, on funding; this rule may do nothing. MEXC public funding history begins only 2025-04-27, so this is strictly a forward risk-control candidate and needs the missing-metrics audit first. |

The first variant uses a cross-sectional regression only on observable lagged features, fit separately at the rebalance; it must be frozen with exact missing-value/tie handling before any returns are scored. If the tradeable MEXC universe leaves too few names in either joint corner, the family is infeasible as written; do not tune the percentiles after inspecting results.

## Trial/data/cost gate

- Latest inspected program count is 9,479,191 in [PANEL](../docs/PANEL.md). If all three candidate families were tested with their stated controls, they would add 7 strategy books, taking the record to 9,479,198 before any additional choices. This count does not erase the prior program-wide search; within-family SPA/BH is also not enough by itself. Select one family or none; count every control and diagnostic.
- No reads of R304's 2024–2026 data can validate these formulas. Use only strictly forward on-time MEXC paper days after a frozen lane has the feature pipeline and cost model, or a truly untouched, survival-safe reserved sample. R304 cannot simply be replayed on 2020 because its OI/positioning features are absent before 2021; a price/volume-only double sort would be a different model and a separately counted family.
- Require point-in-time listings and delistings, as-of top-150 membership, feature timestamps, missingness/coverage report by survival status, exact MEXC contract mapping, observed quotes/depth at the tested size, API fees, funding cash/settlement rules and a short-margin/liquidation model. If a bar or funding rate is unavailable, record it missing, not zero.
- R304's internal cost levels are 8.5/17/25.5 bp per side at 1x/2x/3x. Those may be used only as a replication control; a MEXC candidate must report actual venue cost, funding, turnover, both legs separately, and short-squeeze losses. Mercik's “moderate cost” and the order-flow paper's spot-cost break-even figures are not substitutes for those inputs.
- Do not optimize the regime cutoff, joint-sort cells, funding threshold or short caps on the spent sample. One frozen family, one predeclared primary comparison, one untouched/forward read, then report nulls with the same care as passes.
