# Fixed blends, gates, and the current candidate set — 2026-10-29

This is a read-only audit of existing evidence and input availability. It adds no outcome statistics, does not rerun any lane, and does not alter any frozen rule or forward journal. Existing figures below are reported from the frozen findings/registry, not recomputed here.

## Decision

The smallest credible combination test is two sleeves with **fixed capital weights**, a shared prospective evaluation window, and sleeve-alone / cash-or-passive controls. A 50/50 initial-capital split is the simplest precommitted benchmark; it is not an optimized or recommended final allocation. Measure actual account NAV, both legs’ costs, venue-specific funding cash, peak margin and liquidation paths. Do not combine Sharpes, t-statistics, forecast scores, or archive-standardized family units as if they were jointly invested dollars.

There is no qualified pair and no executable combined test in this checkout today. The raw `data/` tree has no market files beyond a zero-byte lock. R371, R355 and R364 have no scored observations; R285 has no final/scored book days. R304/R305 and the R309/R228 archives are on already-read historical windows. The new paired outcome is therefore unavailable without waiting for future public captures. No paid or historical download is required to *start* a prospective test later, but the data that would make one measurable do not yet exist here.

Do not add a conditional gate now. The current candidates either share the same source of return, have not produced prospective observations, or have unresolved accounting/capital problems. A gate would add timing states and turnover before we have a joint sample that can show its incremental value.

## Fixed blend versus conditional gate

For two sleeve returns on the same decision clock, a fixed portfolio is

`r_port,t = w r_A,t + (1-w) r_B,t - costs_t`,

with `w` fixed before the evaluation period. Its variance contains the covariance term `2w(1-w)Cov(r_A,r_B)`: the number of features or named strategies is not a substitute for measured joint dependence. Bates and Granger’s original forecast-combination study estimated weights from past forecast errors; its result is a reason to examine dependence, not a license to estimate new weights on this project’s already-read windows. DeMiguel, Garlappi and Uppal’s out-of-sample study found no one optimized allocation consistently beat 1/N across its datasets, supporting a simple equal-weight benchmark when evidence is short. Neither paper establishes crypto profitability. [Bates & Granger (1969)](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1057/jors.1969.103), [DeMiguel et al. (2009)](https://academic.oup.com/rfs/article-abstract/22/5/1915/1592901?login=false)

A gate makes the weight state-dependent, e.g. `w_t = g(state_t)`. That adds a state definition, threshold, timing rule, trade entry/exit and the costs of changing exposure. The project’s existing R233 test already found no eligible trend/volatility state to gate the flow books, and the R228/R253 work found no reliable benefit from reweighting those same dependent sleeves. Gârleanu and Pedersen’s primary cost-aware trading model instead derives partial adjustment toward a moving aim from return persistence and trading costs; its commodity-futures result is not a crypto gate, but it illustrates why turnover and signal decay belong in the portfolio rule rather than being treated as free. [Gârleanu & Pedersen (2013)](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/jofi.12080)

Capital weights also must reflect actual obligations. For MEXC, public contract metadata exposes contract size, fees, leverage limits, initial and maintenance margin, liquidation fee and risk tiers; the venue’s liquidation guide says the trigger is based on fair price and margin, with cross-margin exposure shared within an account. These values are contract-, size-, mode- and account-dependent. For Lighter, funding is hourly and uses index-price settlement; standard-account zero fees do not remove spread, taker delay or margin risk. Therefore an equal archive-risk book can require untenable notional or collateral, and a “50% strategy weight” cannot be translated into a 50% notional position without a capital ledger. [MEXC contract-info API](https://www.mexc.io/api-docs/futures/market-endpoints/get-contract-info), [MEXC liquidation FAQ](https://www.mexc.com/support/article/faq-on-liquidation-for-futures-trading-8123281969561), [Lighter funding calculation](https://docs.lighter.xyz/trading/funding#funding-rate-calculation), [Lighter fees](https://docs.lighter.xyz/trading/trading-fees.md)

## Smallest future preregistration that could answer the question

When two independently qualified sleeves have a common prospective window, freeze one two-sleeve, 50/50 initial-capital blend and these controls: each sleeve alone on the same total capital; 50/50 sleeve A plus cash or a risk-matched passive replacement for B; and cash. This is one predeclared candidate, not a weight sweep. Use one date/event join rule with missing or late legs left unknown/ineligible, a finite stopping date, and a paired uncertainty method clustered by day or settlement as appropriate. Report net account return, 1x/2x/3x costs, turnover, drawdown, per-venue cash/margin, peak risk, and both leg-level and portfolio-level liquidation checks. For multi-venue books, reserve capital separately per venue unless actual cross-margin and transfers are proven.

Do not run the comparison until both exact sleeve rules and data pipelines are frozen and produce synchronized point-in-time holdings, fills/quotes, funding cash and NAV on future dates. In this checkout there is no such pair: R371 has no ticket yet; R304 has no frozen MEXC forward port; R355/R364 require accounting/missing-settlement treatment in a new freeze; R285 is already the only active blend lane. Running a new historical blend on R309, R228, or R304 summaries would be exploratory reuse, not new confirmation.

If a gate is later proposed, give it its own preregistration and compare it to the same static blend on the same unseen dates. Freeze one state observable before trading, one threshold, one hysteresis/exit rule, one turnover/cost model, and one test. Do not choose regimes or thresholds by searching the forward stream. No new gate should be added to the current running lanes.

## Primary research read for this review

- Bates, J. M. & Granger, C. W. J. (1969), “The Combination of Forecasts,” *Journal of the Operational Research Society*. Publisher abstract describes composite forecasts and past forecast errors for weights. It is classical point-forecast evidence, not crypto portfolio evidence.
- DeMiguel, V., Garlappi, L. & Uppal, R. (2009), “Optimal Versus Naive Diversification,” *Review of Financial Studies*. Publisher abstract reports 14 allocation models across seven datasets and no consistent out-of-sample dominance over 1/N in Sharpe, certainty-equivalent return, or turnover. Transfer is limited to the allocation-estimation lesson.
- Gârleanu, N. & Pedersen, L. H. (2013), “Dynamic Trading with Predictable Returns and Transaction Costs,” *Journal of Finance*. Publisher/NBER abstract describes an optimal cost-aware policy that partially trades toward an aim portfolio and gives more weight to slower-decaying signals; the empirical application is commodity futures.
- Ledoit, O. & Wolf, M. (2004), “Honey, I Shrunk the Sample Covariance Matrix,” *Journal of Portfolio Management*. The authors’ university record explains why sample covariance estimation error can destabilize mean-variance weights and describes shrinkage. This supports not fitting a covariance optimizer on the tiny prospective sample; it does not set crypto weights. [University record](https://econ-papers.upf.edu/en/onepaper.php?id=691)
