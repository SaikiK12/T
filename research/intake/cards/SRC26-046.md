# Research card: SRC26-046 — Open expected-move rails are a proxy

Source: TrendStoic, [Expected Move Rails (IV-based)](https://www.tradingview.com/script/E5iEoXKs-Expected-Move-Rails-IV-based/), Pine v5, public source revision 5.0. All 98 lines read 2026-10-29; receipt hashes in the [manifest](../reels_tradingview_batch_20261029.json). No compilation, chart or market test. It is not the ATrain/Fractal wall calculation.

## 1. What the source says

The indicator uses one manually entered annual IV and an adjustable divisor, default 16. Rails are an anchor price plus/minus price times IV divided by that divisor. It anchors on the first eligible bar's close, and displays only the last chart day. It has no historical IV series, option chain or trading account. Its latest-day display condition is not a historical forecast signal. The reciprocal divisor approximates square-root annual-to-daily scaling, not a calibrated physical containment probability.

## 2. What we can reproduce

The arithmetic is inspectable. A current manual IV cannot be pasted into past sessions as if known then. A crypto adaptation also needs an explicit 365-day versus business-day convention and matching tenor. A volatility band does not measure dealer inventory or an automatic reversal edge.

## 3. The experiment it earns, or none

Keep this as an M412 level-construction proxy, with M099 context. Before trading, require a timestamped prior-session IV, causal anchor, range-calibration comparison and fixed entry/exit economics. Compare with a realized-volatility band on the same days. Do not label it a replication of IV Walls. Zero new trials or outcomes.
