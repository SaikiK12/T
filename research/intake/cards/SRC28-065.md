# Research card: SRC28-065 — The Natural Gas Announcement Day Puzzle

Source: Marcel Prokopczuk, Chardin Wese Simen and Robert Wichmann, Energy Journal 42(2), 2021. Read the openly posted accepted manuscript from the University of Reading: paper sections II, III.A–B, IV, V.C and VI; Table 8 and Table A.3; PDF pages 1–18, 33 and 42. The other appendix tables were not read.

## 1. What the source says

- **Published result.** The authors study NYMEX Henry Hub NG futures. They build nearby-contract returns from 499 contracts spanning March 2003–December 2018, using a specified monthly roll, and use Bloomberg median EIA storage forecasts.
- **Published result.** The sample has 699 gas-report announcement days and 3,283 non-announcement days after excluding coincident petroleum-report dates. The paper’s reported greater-than-50% contribution is the announcement days’ share of the full-sample annualized natural-gas return; it is not a positive long-only profit claim. The full-sample first-nearby annualized mean return is −31.65%.
- **Published result.** In the two-hour intraday window from 90 minutes before to 30 minutes after the report, the mean return difference is split 49.4% before and 50.6% after. The long sample’s daily announcement effect is not an estimate of today’s effect: Table A.3 shows no announcement/non-announcement mean difference in 2014–2018 (−0.05% versus −0.14%, p=0.642).
- **Published result.** The candidate strategy shorts 90 minutes before the release and covers 30 minutes after. Across the full 2003–2018 sample it earns 12.01% annualized after bid/ask transaction costs and fully funded overnight interest (Sharpe 1.76, p=0.003). In the paper’s post-2011 sample it returns −0.97% after those costs (Sharpe −0.17, p=0.843). The authors describe declining forecasting error and strategy performance.
- **Published result.** Price inputs are daily futures prices plus five-minute intraday data. The implementation uses the last bid to open the short and last ask to cover; it does not model queue position, order-level fills, market impact or a modern venue’s latency.
- **Published result.** Regression standard errors use Newey–West with two lags; Table 8 reports tests of mean strategy returns against zero. The result is historical and does not account for our program’s selection across methods.
- **Published result.** The cited evidence is materially time-dependent. The paper itself reports that the effect was absent in 2014–2018 and that post-2011 strategy returns after costs were negative.
