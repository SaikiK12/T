# Research card: SRC28-066 — EIA order-level liquidity preprint

Source: Ansh Hemang Dani, “Where Does Liquidity Go Around EIA Releases? Order-Level Evidence From CME Energy Futures, With Implications For Backtest Queue Models,” SSRN 7609738, posted 2026-10-14. Read the SSRN abstract only; the 36-page paper body, appendix, code and underlying data were not read.

## 1. What the source says

- **Working-paper result.** The abstract says the author reconstructs NYMEX CL and NG outright books from CME market-by-order data and compares 29 petroleum-report and 31 gas-report releases from March–October 2026 with four matched control days per release, under pre-registered analysis plans.
- **Working-paper result.** It reports that depth within five ticks of mid remained depleted during the first five minutes after the price reaction. It expresses the excess depletion as 33.8 seconds of normal near-mid depth for crude and 48.3 seconds for gas.
- **Working-paper result.** The abstract explicitly says release days are Wednesdays and Thursdays while controls are not, so it cannot separate release effects from Wednesday/Thursday 10:30 clock effects. Several pre-registered placebos failed.
- **Working-paper result.** The abstract reports queue-estimator comparisons, but its displayed P&L results are small and sign-dependent; it does not report a profitable release-entry strategy. Full methods and results remain unverified because only the abstract was read.

## 2. What we can reproduce

- **Working-paper result.** No CME market-by-order file, order-ID history or matching queue ground truth is present in this checkout.
- **Working-paper result.** The local Lighter trade recorder preserves trades and best quotes when trades occur; the existing book script makes hourly snapshots. These do not measure continuous five-minute near-touch depth or order cancellations.
- **Working-paper result.** No fee tier, event-time latency, queue model or executable cost result for our account is established by the abstract.
