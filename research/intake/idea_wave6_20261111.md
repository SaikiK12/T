## Tested in this wave
- **M520, trade.xyz index, commodity and FX perps over the weekend: R464 `experiments/xyzns_wknd_v1`, NULL.**
  - **Mechanics** (lens 3; re-read by the author in trade.xyz `llms-full.txt`, sha256 `7ac7ef34…` [A]):
    - FX: "Internal pricing is used from Friday 5:00 PM to Sunday 5:00 PM ET, or at any time there is a gap of more
      than 30 seconds between external oracle datapoints."
    - Indices and commodities: external prices 23/5 from Sunday 18:00 to Friday 17:00 New York, Brent Friday 18:00
      (Specification Index). The commodities page says 17:00 for all.
    - No separate closed-market rule is written for indices and commodities. The generic one applies: the oracle
      "initializes from the last available external price" and runs a 30-minute EMA.
    - Gold perps are excluded from growth mode ("Perps tracking gold, because PAXG-USDC already tracks gold price").
  - **Result.** Fading weekend moves from an hour before the Sunday reopen to two hours after earns:
    - +2.7 bp at |W| ≥ 25 bp (p 0.86) and +1.9 bp at |W| ≥ 50 bp (p 0.93) in sample A (18 weekends, May-September);
    - net negative from 1x cost.
    - Sample B (6 weekends, October-November) is strongly positive (+75 and +102 bp), but A decides.
  - The stock-perp effect (R462) is therefore not a general trade.xyz weekend effect in A. R432 found the same NULL
    on Binance's index and commodity perps. R463 still tests the stocks forward.

## The outside evidence on weekend overshoot (lens 1, re-checked)
- **Blockworks for the Hyperliquid Policy Center, "Price Discovery in Hyperliquid's Weekend Markets"** (2026-08-03)
  [A, re-read 2026-11-11]:
  - "This report was commissioned by Hyperliquid Policy Center"; the sponsor "may have input on the content".
  - 614 market-weekends across 68 TradeXYZ markets, 2026-03-13 to 2026-07-03, at least $1M of weekend volume each.
  - The reopening move (the median external print 5-10 minutes after the reopen) is the dependent variable. It is
    regressed on the final internal move five minutes before the reopen, both from the Friday close.
  - Slope 0.834 ("the interval consistent with the data, 0.73 to 0.93"), intercept -15 bp. The weekend move accounts
    for 69% of the variation. Execution figures are "gross of fees".
- **Correction to the helper's reading.** The lens 1 helper read the slope below 1 as under-reaction. With the
  reopening move as the dependent variable, a slope below 1 means the reopen moves less than the perp did, so the
  perp gives back about a sixth of its weekend move. That is the overshoot R462 trades.
  - Part of that slope can be noise in the weekend price, which also pulls a slope below 1. A fade earns that noise
    too, because it is in the price a trader pays.
- **Blockworks, February-March 2026** (6 weekends, 199 symbol-weekends) [V]: slope 0.816. The authors say moves
  largely carry through rather than revert toward Friday's close, which is the same fact read from the other side.
- **IMF Global Financial Stability Report, October 2026, chapter 3** (via CryptoSlate) [V, secondary]: off-hours moves
  of tokenized stocks pass through 87-99% to the next session.
- **Binance Research, "Beyond the Closing Bell"** (via Finance Magnates) [V, secondary]:
  - median capture of Monday's move 92% over seven weekends;
  - one SEC-news episode where perps overshot (median priced-in ratio 140%).
- **Cong, Landsman, Rabetti, Zhang, Zhao** (tokenized-stock deck, Bank of Finland, May 2026) [S]: "1% ⇒ +0.28%
  close-to-open". The deck was not readable.
- **Missing everywhere [U]:** a net-of-cost fade or follow result, and any reversion measured after the first hour
  past the reopen.

## Where each other idea sits
- **Crypto-equity weekend lead-lag** (lens 6).
  - Mourey, Shahrour and Şoiman, "A crypto-stock weekend effect", Finance Research Letters 86 (2025), DOI
    10.1016/j.frl.2025.108661. The abstract was re-read [A]:
    - negative weekend crypto returns "significantly predict Monday equity declines, while positive returns have no
      effect";
    - robust across Nasdaq, Russell 2000 and S&P sectors, and stronger after the May 2022 LUNA collapse.
    - Slopes, sample and costs: [U], since the full text was blocked.
  - The lab's M198 (BTC weekend return to the NQ Monday gap) is R244 NULL on QQQ.
  - On a 24/7 venue the stock perp already trades at the weekend. The open question is narrower: does a crypto-linked
    perp (COIN, HOOD, MSTR, CRCL, BMNR, IREN on xyz) under-reflect BTC's weekend move, so that the residual β·r_BTC
    minus its own weekend move predicts the reopen?
  - R462 already read these markets' weekend prices in aggregate, so a historical test is not blind. New row **M521
    (UNTESTED)**: about 6 markets × 24 weekends; the effective N is near the number of weekends.
- **Month-end rebalancing** (lens 4).
  - Harvey, Mazzoleni and Melone, "The Unintended Consequences of Rebalancing", NBER w33554 (March 2025, revised
    January 2026) [A]: when stocks are overweight, funds sell them, "leading to a decrease in equity returns of 17
    basis points over the next day"; the authors put the cost at about $16 billion a year.
  - On a 24/7 index perp (xyz SP500, XYZ100) the trade is a short into the predictable selling. Only about 8
    month-ends exist on xyz, so the signal needs long daily cash history (SPY and a bond ETF).
  - New row **M522 (UNTESTED)**: the 17 bp is the authors' in-sample figure; after costs and publication it may be
    smaller.
- **End-of-day leveraged-ETF rebalancing and intraday momentum** (lens 4).
  - Barbon, Beckmeyer, Buraschi and Moerke [V, abstract]: rebalancing "induce[s] significant end-of-day momentum and
    mean-reversion"; the effects "dissipate within the next trading day" and are "decreasing significantly over
    time".
  - Fed FEDS 2013-48 [S]: about $1.04 billion of rebalancing flow per 1% index move.
  - New row **M523 (UNTESTED)**: the day's index move to 15:00 New York predicts the last hour, on xyz SP500 and XYZ100
    hourly bars. The expected gain is a few bp, so the 5.8 bp round trip likely binds.
- **Overnight versus intraday** (lens 5).
  - NY Fed Liberty Street, "The Disappearing Overnight Drift" (2026-07-01) [A]:
    - the 2-3 a.m. ET E-mini window earned about 3.7% a year over 1998-2020 (5,691 days);
    - it "has averaged close to zero since 2021" (1,245 days to December 2025); NQ and YM are flat too.
    - NightShares' overnight ETFs (NSPY, NIWM) closed about fourteen months after their June 2022 launch, "consistent
      with the weakened pattern".
  - Mamaysky et al. (arXiv 2507.04481) [V, abstract] still find overnight gains over 30 years.
  - At growth-mode fees a nightly round trip costs about 1.8 bp before slippage, against a historical edge near
    1.5 bp a night.
  - The lab's R235 (M195, QQQ) and R435 (M492, MEXC cross-section) cover it. No new row.
- **Cross-venue weekend pairs** (lens 2).
  - OKX [V]: "OKX supports 24/7 trading for stock perpetuals"; off-hours the index is held within 10% of the last
    stock price and leans on OKX's own contract.
  - Lighter [V]: RWA prices go internal "outside the underlying's trading hours"; 1-hour funding; 0/0 standard fees.
  - Kraken xStocks are not available in the EEA [V]; Bybit TradFi is not available in the EEA [S]; Ostium pauses
    execution when closed [S].
  - No pair with free weekend history was confirmed. The idea is M443's (cross-venue residual), already UNTESTED.
- **Russell reconstitution and index effective dates** (lens 4).
  - Dimensional (2014-2023) [V]: migrating Russell 2000 deletions fall about 1.1% before and recover about 2% over 20
    days; additions mirror it.
  - BMLL [V]: 34% of the 2024 Russell day's notional traded in the closing auction.
  - One event a year, for few perp-listed names: context, next to M519.
- **Option expiry week and triple witching** (lens 4): Stivers and Sun (2013) [S]; Schaeffer's (-0.29% per
  triple-witching week since 2021, about 14-20 events) [S]. Too few events for a test. Context.
- **Buyback blackout windows** (lens 4): Alpha Architect (2019) [S] finds no strong link to market performance.
  Context.
